\section{Argument soundness}
\label{sec:soundness}

This section gives a concrete classical random-oracle argument-soundness
bound for the relation in Definition~\ref{def:spend-relation}.  The
finite-parameter code reduction and the state-restoration property needed by
the Fiat--Shamir compiler are stated explicitly as premises below.  Section~\ref{sec:knowledge-soundness} states, under an added round-by-round knowledge premise, the extractor that upgrades this to an argument of knowledge and the theft-resistance corollary that follows.

\subsection{Security experiment and metric}

Let \(\Pi_{\mathrm{spend}}\) denote the interactive public-coin protocol obtained by
replacing each Fiat--Shamir challenge with an independent verifier message.
The adversary may choose the public pre-state and statement \(x\), receive the
verifier messages adaptively, and return all oracle messages and openings.  It
wins if the verifier accepts while
\(
  \{w:\RProfile(x,w)=1\}=\varnothing
\).
Account-state rejection can only reduce this event, so the algebraic analysis
may conservatively omit the direct pre-state checks.

\begin{remark}[Statement-encoding change]
The v4 statement encoding extends the statement-digest preimage by the
32-byte deployment domain of Definition~\ref{def:spend-statement}.  The
digest remains one 32-byte SHA-256 value absorbed at the same transcript
position, so the Fiat--Shamir event ledger below is unchanged: no absorb
boundary, challenge, or grinding predicate is added or moved by the new
field.
\end{remark}

For the Fiat--Shamir protocol, let a classical adversary make
\(T\in[1,2^{128}]\) queries to the 256-bit random oracle, including queries
made after it has produced candidate proof bytes.  We report
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Adv}^{\mathsf{snd}}_{\mathrm{spend}}(T)
 =\frac{\Pr[\text{the adversary produces an accepting false statement}]}{T},
 \label{eq:work-normalized-soundness}
\end{equation}
the success probability per random-oracle query.  This normalization is
appropriate for a protocol whose explicit grinding work is implemented by
random-oracle search, and it is the metric used for the numerical claim.

\subsection{Code transport and list binding}

Write
\[
 \mathcal C'=
 \{p|_{G'_{19}}:p\in L'_{10}(\mathbb K)\}.
\]
This is the exact 1,024-coefficient subcode evaluated on the
\(2^{19}\)-symbol circle coset.  S-two Theorem~5 and
Equations~(21)--(22) give \(\dim L'_{10}=2^{10}\) and
\(L'_{10}\subseteq L_{10}\).  Its Definition~6, Lemma~1, and Section~1.4
identify the ambient \(L_{10}\) circle code, under the rational circle
parametrization and a nonzero coordinate scaling, with a generalized
Reed--Solomon code of degree at most 1,024; its Theorem~7 supplies the
corresponding Johnson-regime list-decoding result
\cite{CarmonEtAl2026Stwo}.  Coordinate permutation and nonzero diagonal
scaling preserve Hamming distance, agreement sets, and list sizes.  Passing
to \(\mathcal C'\) can only shrink a decoding list.

\begin{lemma}[Circle-to-GRS transport]
\label{lem:circle-grs-transport}
For the \(2^{10}\)-coefficient, \(2^{19}\)-symbol \Profile{} circle code,
the implemented circle-to-line map identifies each relevant affine code
slice with a scaled GRS slice having the same relative distance and the same
agreement sets.  The four implemented arity-four fiber folds commute with
this identification.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The circle parametrization pairs inverse points into the implemented
arity-four fibers.  Restricting a polynomial to the normalized circle and
then applying \(t=2x^2-1\) gives the line evaluation used by the verifier.
On the selected coset the 131,072 fiber roots are distinct and none is one;
the release evaluator checks all of them.  Evaluation therefore differs
from the corresponding GRS evaluation only by the fixed order and nonzero
normalizers.  Expanding the implemented fold gives the degree-three powers
generator \(a_0+\alpha a_1+\alpha^2a_2+\alpha^3a_3\); the coordinate
divisions are the invertible local FFT change of basis.  S-two Lemma~4 and
WHIR Lemma~4.13/Theorem~4.20 then give fold/list commutation for this powers
generator, while the terminal constraint transports the linear subcode
condition~\cite{CarmonEtAl2026Stwo,ArnonChiesaFenziYogev2025WHIR}.
\end{proof}

The soundness ledger uses proven Johnson list decoding and the
polynomial-generator MCA regime, rather than a capacity conjecture.  The
required multicodeword-agreement bounds are the one-domain specialization of
S-two Protocol~3, Theorem~19, and Lemmas~3--4.  For the scalar-powers
generator, the corresponding Reed--Solomon MCA bound is Bordage et al.,
Definition~9.1 and Lemma~9.3; their Theorem~9.2 extends the result to every
polynomial generator
\cite{CarmonEtAl2026Stwo,BordageChiesaGuanManzur2025,Haboeck2025MCA}.

\begin{lemma}[Johnson/MCA batching]
\label{lem:johnson-mca-batch}
Suppose the polynomial-batching, OOD, and local algebraic bad events listed in
Table~\ref{tab:soundness-events} do not occur.  Then the two layer-zero
commitments and their \(\gamma\)-batch in
Equation~\eqref{eq:spend-batch} determine one member of the transported
rate-\(1/512\) code list, and every accepted fold and terminal value belongs
to the corresponding folded list.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The width-29 scalar-power batch is a polynomial-generator family.  Outside
its collision event, the MCA theorem reduces simultaneous proximity of the
committed lanes to proximity of their single \(\gamma\)-combination.  The
rate-\(1/512\) parameters lie inside the published proven-Johnson regime.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:circle-grs-transport}, the bound transfers to the implemented
circle code.  Linear arity-four folding carries every candidate list element
to the next list.  The two OOD samples and relation sumcheck at each layer
bind the claimed list member; their exceptional events are charged
separately in the ledger.  Finally, the four-coefficient terminal check fixes
the last folded polynomial.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Local algebraic binding]
\label{lem:local-algebraic-binding}
Conditioned on the code/list conclusion of
Lemma~\ref{lem:johnson-mca-batch}, an accepting transcript either encodes a
trace satisfying Proposition~\ref{prop:relation-realization}, or one of the
local events in rows 5--14 of Table~\ref{tab:soundness-events} occurs.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The relation OOD mixers bind the batched semantic, copy, lookup, and mask
identities.  A nonzero \(\gamma\) separates the 29 generator lanes and the
three statement points.  Tagged-tuple compression, copy denominators, and the
\(\theta\)-batch are sound outside their displayed collision or pole events.
The zerocheck equality point and ten degree-27 rounds bind the trace
constraints, while the \(H_1\) and nonzero-\(\eta\) rows bind the masked
sumcheck.  When these events are absent, all constraints and public terminal
equalities hold, so Proposition~\ref{prop:relation-realization} supplies a
valid witness.
\end{proof}

The preceding proofs identify the intended reduction, but the generated
calculator and regression fixtures are not a formal verification of every
imported-theorem hypothesis.  The concrete theorem therefore isolates the
finite-parameter step rather than silently treating executable agreement as
a proof.

\begin{assumption}[Pinned finite-parameter reduction]
\label{ass:pinned-finite-reduction}
For the exact code, transcript, and verifier serialized by the frozen
artifact, Lemmas~\ref{lem:circle-grs-transport}--
\ref{lem:local-algebraic-binding} hold with the event partition and bounds in
Table~\ref{tab:soundness-events}.  In particular, the premise covers the
rate-\(1/512\) Johnson-list and polynomial-generator MCA hypotheses, the two
OOD samples, all four folds and their list sizes, the terminal condition, and
the assignment of every semantic, copy, lookup, range, mask, and sumcheck
constraint to rows 5--14 of the table.  The premise is uniform over every
false public statement and every reachable public transcript prefix.
\end{assumption}

\subsection{Selector scope}

The selector byte is absorbed only after both commitments, all OOD data,
folds, the terminal polynomial, and the final work nonce have been fixed.
Let \(B\) denote the union of all bad events determined before that byte, and
let \(M_b\) be the final query-miss event for branch \(b\).

\begin{lemma}[Three-branch selector containment]
\label{lem:selector-soundness}
For every possibly adversarial function that selects
\(b\in\{0,1,2\}\) after observing all three schedules,
\[
 B\lor M_b\quad\Longrightarrow\quad B\lor M_0\lor M_1\lor M_2.
\]
Consequently the three-branch construction changes only the final query-miss
term by a factor of at most three; it does not replicate any common-prefix
term.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The implication is pointwise and does not require independence between the
selector and the schedules.  The common-prefix transcript replay additionally
checks equality of the prefix challenges, OOD points, mixers, fold challenges,
and pre-final-grinding state across all branches.  A union bound over
\(M_0,M_1,M_2\) completes the argument.
\end{proof}

The release theorem below takes the still more conservative course of
multiplying the complete transformed ledger by three.  This avoids relying on
the tighter scope allocation in its headline value.

\subsection{Fiat--Shamir instantiation}

The BCS compiler is characterized by soundness against state-restoration
attacks, which is stronger than ordinary interactive soundness
\cite{BenSassonChiesaSpooner2016,BlockGarretaKatzThalerTiwariZajac2023}.
A state-restoration adversary may save the verifier and oracle state at a
public-message boundary, restore a saved state, and request a fresh
continuation; its win event is acceptance of a false statement on any
resulting branch.  Let \(\varepsilon_{\rm sr}\) denote the maximum win
probability in the exact 32-boundary experiment for the serialized
transcript.

\begin{assumption}[Boundary state restoration]
\label{ass:boundary-restoration}
For every false statement, every reachable saved state at each of the 32
boundaries, and every classical state-restoration adversary, the conditional
escape events are covered by the event partition in
Assumption~\ref{ass:pinned-finite-reduction}.  Consequently
\[
 \varepsilon_{\rm sr}\le\varepsilon_{\rm round}^{(0)}.
\]
This is a round-by-round premise; it is not inferred from standard
interactive soundness.  Section~\ref{sec:rbr-discharge} discharges the generic
boundaries of this premise, reducing it to the strictly smaller
Assumption~\ref{ass:relation-rbr-core}.
\end{assumption}

We use the BCS Fiat--Shamir analysis for IOPs
\cite{BenSassonChiesaSpooner2016}, in the explicit bounded-query form of
S-two Theorem~22~\cite{CarmonEtAl2026Stwo}.  Under
Assumption~\ref{ass:boundary-restoration}, its instantiation with \(R=32\)
message boundaries and \(\lambda=256\) gives
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{BCS}}(T)
 \le (T+R)\varepsilon_{\rm round}^{(0)}
       +\frac{3(T^2+1)}{2^\lambda}.
 \label{eq:bcs-raw}
\end{equation}
At the upper query budget, the right-hand side of
Equation~\eqref{eq:bcs-raw} exceeds one and is therefore vacuous as a bound
on raw success probability.  The released claim is only the explicitly
defined work-normalized metric in
Equation~\eqref{eq:work-normalized-soundness}; it does not book the raw bound
as a security floor.
Dividing by \(T\) gives the metric in
Equation~\eqref{eq:work-normalized-soundness}:
\begin{equation}
 b(T):=\frac{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{BCS}}(T)}{T}
 \le
 \left(1+\frac{32}{T}\right)\varepsilon_{\rm round}^{(0)}
 +\frac{3(T+1/T)}{2^{256}}.
 \label{eq:bcs-normalized}
\end{equation}
At \(T=1\), the multiplier of the round error is therefore 33.  The
right-hand side is convex for positive \(T\), so its maximum on
\([1,2^{128}]\) is attained at an endpoint.  Direct evaluation yields
\[
 -\log_2 b(1)=101.74641188359571,
 \qquad
 -\log_2 b(2^{128})=106.79080421773332.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Work-normalized classical-ROM soundness]
\label{thm:bcs-soundness}
Assume the pinned finite-parameter reduction
(Assumption~\ref{ass:pinned-finite-reduction}), the 32-boundary
state-restoration property (Assumption~\ref{ass:boundary-restoration}), and
SHA-256 modeled as a 256-bit random oracle.  For every classical adversary making
\(1\le T\le2^{128}\) oracle queries,
\[
 \operatorname{Adv}^{\mathsf{snd}}_{\mathrm{spend}}(T)
 \le 3b(T)\le 2^{-100.16144938287455}.
\]
Thus \Profile{} has a conservative 100.161-bit argument-soundness floor in
the work-normalized metric.
\end{theorem}

The work-normalized floor is a per-query bound.  The cumulative raw advantage
at a fixed query budget is \(3\varepsilon_{\mathrm{BCS}}(T)\); it grows with
\(T\) and becomes vacuous as \(T\) approaches the round error, as every
grinding-based argument does.  Table~\ref{tab:raw-advantage} reports both so
the metric is not misread as a raw \(2^{-100}\) forgery probability at every
budget.

factor gives 100.16144938287455 and 105.20584171701216 bits, respectively;
the former is the minimum.  Every accepted false statement is included in
this event by the preceding lemmas.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Round-by-round discharge of the generic boundaries}
\label{sec:rbr-discharge}

Assumption~\ref{ass:boundary-restoration} bundles two different things: the
generic Fiat--Shamir plumbing of a FRI/WHIR-style compiler --- commitment,
sumcheck, fold, grinding, query, and selector boundaries --- and the bespoke
relation binding of the \Profile{} encoding and its zero-factor \(D\) lane.
The generic part is not specific to Aspis; it is discharged here from imported
round-by-round soundness, given the pinned finite-parameter reduction.  The
bespoke part is isolated as a strictly smaller residual.  Throughout, the
per-boundary doom-persistence error \(\varepsilon_i\) is the maximum, over
reachable restored doomed prefixes and the false statement, of the probability
that the next challenge \(c_i\) flips the state function from doomed to
un-doomed.

\begin{lemma}[Generic-boundary state-restoration soundness]
\label{lem:generic-boundary-rbr}
Grant Assumption~\ref{ass:pinned-finite-reduction}.  For the commitment-root,
masked-sumcheck, fold-challenge, grinding, two-sample out-of-domain, query-draw, and
three-branch-selector boundaries, \(\varepsilon_i\) is at most the
corresponding entry of Table~\ref{tab:soundness-events}, uniformly over every
false statement and reachable saved state.  Concretely: a Merkle root fixes its
committed vector before its public-coin challenge, giving commit-then-sample
persistence~\citep{BenSassonChiesaSpooner2016}; the sumcheck rounds are
round-by-round sound with per-round error \(27/|\mathbb K|\); the arity-four
circle folds are identified with scaled GRS folds by
Lemma~\ref{lem:circle-grs-transport} and inherit the folding round-by-round
soundness of \citet{ArnonChiesaFenziYogev2025WHIR} in the Fiat--Shamir form
of \citet{BlockGarretaKatzThalerTiwariZajac2023}; each \(b\)-bit grinding
predicate persists doom except with \(2^{-b}\) per query; the query draw
inherits the FRI query-phase bound at the transported rate-\(1/512\) Johnson
agreement cap of Assumption~\ref{ass:pinned-finite-reduction} (agreement
\(A=6082\)), which is what that bound consumes; and the selector boundary is
governed by the already-proved Lemma~\ref{lem:selector-soundness}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each item instantiates the cited round-by-round result at the pinned
parameters; the algebraic hypotheses each needs --- the list/MCA regime, the
circle-to-GRS transport, the agreement cap, the terminal degree --- are the
content of Assumption~\ref{ass:pinned-finite-reduction}, so the fold, query,
and out-of-domain items are conditional on that retained premise and are not
claimed unconditionally.  The circle-code case is reached only through
Lemma~\ref{lem:circle-grs-transport}, not by a literal instantiation of the
multiplicative-coset statements.  The selector item is
Lemma~\ref{lem:selector-soundness}, which is proved, not assumed.  Uniformity
over restored states holds because each cited state function depends only on
the committed vectors and public challenges already in the restored prefix.
\end{proof}

\begin{assumption}[Relation-binding round-by-round core]
\label{ass:relation-rbr-core}
Grant Assumption~\ref{ass:pinned-finite-reduction}.  For the residual
boundaries --- the width-29 \(\gamma\)-batch of the 26 subfield lanes and the
three extension lanes \(H,G,D\) including the zero-factor \(D\) lane of
Equation~\eqref{eq:spend-batch}, the three \(D\)-lane claims absorbed beside
the statement points, the relation out-of-domain mixers, and the atomic
tuple-compression, copy/range-pole, and \(\theta\) seams --- the
doom-persistence error is at most the corresponding entry of
Table~\ref{tab:soundness-events} (rows for the semantic, copy, lookup, range,
mask, and inactive-copy events, and the \(D\)-lane contributions), uniformly
over every false statement and reachable saved state.  This residual is not
covered by Lemma~\ref{lem:generic-boundary-rbr} nor by any imported
Fiat--Shamir soundness result; it is the irreducible round-by-round premise of
the \Profile{} relation and its zero-factor \(D\) lane.
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}[Boundary-restoration reduction]
\label{lem:boundary-restoration-reduction}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:pinned-finite-reduction} and
\ref{ass:relation-rbr-core} and Lemma~\ref{lem:generic-boundary-rbr}, every
boundary has doom-persistence error at most its tabulated entry, so
\(\varepsilon_{\rm sr}\le\varepsilon_{\rm round}^{(0)}\), the conclusion of
Assumption~\ref{ass:boundary-restoration}.  The two premises may therefore
replace Assumption~\ref{ass:boundary-restoration} in
Theorem~\ref{thm:bcs-soundness}, with \(\varepsilon_{\rm round}^{(0)}\) and the
100.161-bit floor unchanged.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every boundary of the serialized schedule lies in exactly one class of
Lemma~\ref{lem:generic-boundary-rbr} or Assumption~\ref{ass:relation-rbr-core};
summing the per-boundary errors over the whole-transcript state function bounds
the state-restoration win by the union of the tabulated events, which is
\(\varepsilon_{\rm round}^{(0)}\) by
Equation~\eqref{eq:coarse-interactive-error}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Value conservation}
\label{sec:value-conservation}

Theft resistance concerns who may spend a note; value conservation concerns
whether a spend can create value.  The relation carries the balance constraint
\eqref{eq:balance} and the two 30-bit range decompositions
\eqref{eq:range-in}--\eqref{eq:range-out}; the following states their
consequence as a soundness statement.  Let \(\varepsilon_{\rm bind}\) denote
the Poseidon2 collision reserve of the ledger for
Equations~\eqref{eq:input-note} and~\eqref{eq:output-note}.

\begin{theorem}[Value conservation]
\label{thm:value-conservation}
For any accepting spend with public fee \(f\in\{0,\dots,2^{30}-1\}\), with
work-normalized soundness error \(3b(T)\) and additional binding error
\(\varepsilon_{\rm bind}\), there exist integers \(v,v'\in\{0,\dots,2^{30}-1\}\)
such that the consumed input note commits to value \(v\), the created output
note commits to value \(v'\), and
\[
 v' + f = v \qquad\text{over the integers.}
\]
In particular the balance cannot be met by a modular wraparound, and no
accepting spend increases value: the output value plus the public fee equals
the input value.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:bcs-soundness}, with work-normalized error \(3b(T)\) (raw
failure \(3\varepsilon_{\mathrm{BCS}}(T)=3T\,b(T)\), subject to the
Table~\ref{tab:raw-advantage} caveat), acceptance implies a witness \(w\) with
\(\RProfile(x,w)=1\); write \(v,v'\) and the range limbs of \(w\).  Constraints \eqref{eq:range-in}--\eqref{eq:range-out} equate \(v\) and
\(v'\) to their base-\(2^{10}\) recompositions, and each of the six limbs lies
in \(\{0,\dots,2^{10}-1\}\) by the 10-bit lookup argument, so
\(v,v'\in\{0,\dots,2^{30}-1\}\).  Constraint \eqref{eq:balance} gives
\(v'+f\equiv v \pmod p\) with \(p=2^{31}-1\).  Since
\(v',f\le2^{30}-1\), we have \(v'+f\le2^{31}-2=p-1<p\), and \(v\le2^{30}-1<p\);
two residues in \(\{0,\dots,p-1\}\) that are congruent mod \(p\) are equal, so
\(v'+f=v\) over the integers, with no wraparound.  The bound is tight by
construction: \(2(2^{30}-1)=p-1\), so the two 30-bit operands leave zero slack,
and the 30-bit range checks are exactly what force the field balance to be an
integer balance.  Finally, by collision
resistance of \(H_{\mathsf{note}}\) (reserve \(\varepsilon_{\rm bind}\)) the
committed openings \eqref{eq:input-note} and \eqref{eq:output-note} are the
unique values \(v,v'\), so the input note carries value \(v\) and the output
note value \(v'=v-f\le v\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No inflation, relative to initialization]
\label{cor:no-inflation}
Under the premises of Theorem~\ref{thm:value-conservation}, each spend changes
exactly one leaf and replaces an in-tree note of value \(v\) by a note of value
\(v-f\), removing the public fee \(f\) from the shielded set; the total
committed note value is therefore non-increasing across any sequence of
accepting spends.  This is relative to the value committed at initialization.
The pool anchor is a caller-supplied value at initialization and this release
has no deposit or append operation (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}), so the
statement is that spends do not mint value, not that the pool's committed value
is externally backed; establishing an honestly-valued initial anchor, or a
deposit path, is out of scope.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Knowledge soundness and theft resistance}
\label{sec:knowledge-soundness}

Theorem~\ref{thm:bcs-soundness} is a statement about the \emph{existence} of a
satisfying witness.  For a spend, the property a pool needs is stronger: only a
party that knows a note's authorization secret should be able to produce an
accepting transition that consumes it.  We record the additional premise under
which the argument becomes an argument of knowledge, and the theft-resistance
statement that follows from it and from the relation's binding structure.  Like
the soundness premises, the knowledge premise is assumed, not proved.

\begin{assumption}[State-restoration knowledge extraction]
\label{ass:sr-knowledge}
There is an expected-polynomial-time extractor \(\mathsf{Ext}\) with the
following property.  For the exact code, transcript, and verifier of the frozen
artifact, and for every classical state-restoration prover \(P^\ast\) that from
any reachable saved state at each of the 32 boundaries drives the interactive
protocol to an accepting terminal with probability exceeding
\(\varepsilon_{\rm round}^{(0)}\), \(\mathsf{Ext}\), given \(P^\ast\) together
with its transcript of random-oracle queries and answers, outputs codeword
openings and constraint assignments that satisfy every pinned event of
Table~\ref{tab:soundness-events}, except with probability at most
\(\varepsilon_{\rm round}^{(0)}\).  This is the round-by-round knowledge
analogue of Assumption~\ref{ass:boundary-restoration}; it is not inferred from
Theorem~\ref{thm:bcs-soundness}.  It is deliberately heavier than its
soundness sibling: where Assumption~\ref{ass:boundary-restoration} is a scalar
probability bound, this premise is existential and algorithmic --- it posits
both the extractor and its success --- and so is close to the interactive
knowledge-soundness conclusion itself.  Lemma~\ref{lem:sr-knowledge-reduction}
below discharges its existential and algorithmic content, reducing it to the
static algebraic Assumption~\ref{ass:relation-knowledge-core}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}[Generic straight-line message extraction]
\label{lem:generic-extraction}
Grant Assumption~\ref{ass:pinned-finite-reduction} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:generic-boundary-rbr}.  There is a straight-line extractor
\(\mathsf{Ext}_0\) that, given a state-restoration prover \(P^\ast\) with its
random-oracle query--answer transcript, outputs the Merkle-committed layer-zero
and per-layer oracle messages opened at the sampled query positions whenever
\(P^\ast\) accepts, using no rewinding and failing only on a Merkle
second-preimage, of probability at most the SHA-256 reserve.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is the Fiat--Shamir-of-IOP knowledge extractor of
\citet{BenSassonChiesaSpooner2016}: the compiled proof is public-coin with
Merkle-committed oracle messages, so the committed words are read off
\(P^\ast\)'s query transcript at the parser-fixed boundaries, and recovering a
value inconsistent with its authenticating path is a second preimage.  The
generic-boundary soundness of Lemma~\ref{lem:generic-boundary-rbr} supplies the
state function BCS needs for the extractor to be straight-line.
\end{proof}

\begin{assumption}[Relation-witness reconstruction core]
\label{ass:relation-knowledge-core}
Grant Assumptions~\ref{ass:pinned-finite-reduction} and
\ref{ass:relation-rbr-core}.  For the messages recovered by \(\mathsf{Ext}_0\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:generic-extraction}) from any state-restoration prover
accepting with probability exceeding \(\varepsilon_{\rm round}^{(0)}\), the
opened codeword values and the pinned semantic, copy, lookup, range, mask, and
zero-factor-\(D\) assignments satisfy every event of
Table~\ref{tab:soundness-events}, and hence
Proposition~\ref{prop:relation-realization}, except with probability at most
\(\varepsilon_{\rm round}^{(0)}\).  This is an algebraic reconstruction statement about
recovered openings; unlike Assumption~\ref{ass:sr-knowledge} it posits neither
an extractor nor its running time, both of which
Lemma~\ref{lem:generic-extraction} supplies.
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}[State-restoration knowledge reduction]
\label{lem:sr-knowledge-reduction}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:pinned-finite-reduction},
\ref{ass:relation-rbr-core}, and \ref{ass:relation-knowledge-core}, the
extractor \(\mathsf{Ext}=\Phi\circ\mathsf{Ext}_0\), with \(\Phi\) the pinned
encoding of Section~\ref{sec:construction}, meets the conclusion of
Assumption~\ref{ass:sr-knowledge}: it is expected polynomial time and outputs
codeword openings and constraint assignments satisfying every pinned event
except with probability at most \(\varepsilon_{\rm round}^{(0)}\).  The three
premises may therefore replace Assumption~\ref{ass:sr-knowledge} in
Theorem~\ref{thm:argument-of-knowledge}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\(\mathsf{Ext}_0\) is straight-line and linear-time in the transcript
(Lemma~\ref{lem:generic-extraction}), and \(\Phi\) is a fixed deterministic
map; correctness of the composed output is
Assumption~\ref{ass:relation-knowledge-core}, and the message-recovery and
reconstruction failures union to \(\varepsilon_{\rm round}^{(0)}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional non-interactive argument of knowledge]
\label{thm:argument-of-knowledge}
Assume Assumptions~\ref{ass:pinned-finite-reduction},
\ref{ass:boundary-restoration}, and~\ref{ass:sr-knowledge}, and SHA-256 as a
programmable random oracle.  There is a straight-line extractor \(E\) such that
for every prover that outputs an accepting proof \(\pi\) for a statement \(x\)
while making \(T\le2^{128}\) oracle queries, \(E\), given that prover's
query--answer transcript, outputs \(w\) with \(\RProfile(x,w)=1\), with the
same work-normalized knowledge error \(3b(T)\) as the soundness floor of
Theorem~\ref{thm:bcs-soundness}.  As there, the raw failure probability is
\(3\varepsilon_{\mathrm{BCS}}(T)=3T\,b(T)\), subject to the same query-budget
caveat as Table~\ref{tab:raw-advantage}; the \(2^{-100.161}\) figure is the
per-query floor, not a raw probability at every budget.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The BCS Fiat--Shamir transform of a state-restoration interactive oracle proof
is an argument of knowledge in the random-oracle model
\citep{BenSassonChiesaSpooner2016}: the extractor recovers the Merkle-committed
oracle messages from the prover's query transcript, and by
Assumption~\ref{ass:sr-knowledge} their openings at the sampled positions
determine codeword openings and assignments satisfying the pinned constraints.
The pinned encoding of Section~\ref{sec:construction} maps that interactive
witness to a relation witness \(w\) for \(\RProfile\).  Extraction is
straight-line from the query transcript, so it uses no rewinding.  Its failure
event --- a satisfiable statement is accepted but \(\mathsf{Ext}\) fails to
recover the openings --- is logically distinct from the soundness escape event
of Theorem~\ref{thm:bcs-soundness}, in which a false statement is accepted;
Assumption~\ref{ass:sr-knowledge} bounds it by the same
\(\varepsilon_{\rm round}^{(0)}\), so the \(3b(T)\) value carries to the
knowledge error by that premise rather than by the soundness theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Authorization possession and theft resistance]
\label{cor:theft-resistance}
Consider a note created with secret \(k_\nu\) and input randomness
\(r_{\mathrm{in}}\), so that its public nullifier is
\(\nu=H_{\mathsf{nul}}(k_\nu,r_{\mathrm{in}})\) by
Equation~\eqref{eq:nullifier}.  The nullifier is the only witness-derived value
pinned in the public statement, so it alone identifies the note being
consumed.  Let \(A\) be any probabilistic polynomial-time party that outputs an
accepting spend for a statement carrying \(\nu\) while making \(T\le2^{128}\)
oracle queries.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:argument-of-knowledge}, with
work-normalized knowledge error \(3b(T)\), the extractor yields a witness whose
secret \(k\) and randomness \(r'\) satisfy \(H_{\mathsf{nul}}(k,r')=\nu\).
Since
\((k_\nu,r_{\mathrm{in}})\) is a fixed preimage of the public target \(\nu\),
any \((k,r')\neq(k_\nu,r_{\mathrm{in}})\) with the same image is a
second preimage; by the Poseidon2 semantic-binding reserve
\(\varepsilon_{\rm cr}\) of the soundness ledger, which upper-bounds
second-preimage advantage, \((k,r')=(k_\nu,r_{\mathrm{in}})\) except with
probability \(\varepsilon_{\rm cr}\).  The extracted witness therefore contains
the note's secret \(k_\nu\), and, through Equations~\eqref{eq:owner-key} and
\eqref{eq:input-note}, its owner key and input note as well.  Hence any
efficient party that produces an accepting spend of the note knows its
authorization secret, and no efficient party lacking \(k_\nu\) produces an
accepting spend, with work-normalized knowledge error \(3b(T)\) and additional
raw error \(\varepsilon_{\rm cr}\).
\end{corollary}

Corollary~\ref{cor:theft-resistance} bounds an adversary that produces an
accepting spend without being handed honest proofs.  A deployed pool is
stronger: the adversary also observes honest spends.  We close this case by adapting the non-malleability template for the
Fiat--Shamir transform of a proof of knowledge with unique
responses~\citep{FaustKohlweissMarsonVenturi2012} to our setting, and giving
the reduction explicitly.  That template is stated for three-move protocols;
the multi-round case has been treated in the algebraic group
model~\citep{GaneshOrlandiPancholiTakahashiTschudi2022}, which our hash-based
random-oracle argument does not use.  We therefore do not invoke either as a
black box: our two ingredients are that the compiled argument is a proof of
knowledge in the random-oracle model (Theorem~\ref{thm:argument-of-knowledge},
whose Fiat--Shamir foundation for this FRI-style compiler is
\citet{BlockGarretaKatzThalerTiwariZajac2023}, giving round-by-round soundness,
not extractability), and that it has weak unique responses, established next;
the non-malleability step is the explicit straight-line reduction in the proof
of Theorem~\ref{thm:sim-extractability}.

\begin{lemma}[Weak unique response]
\label{lem:quasi-unique-response}
Fix a statement \(x\), the committed hiding pre-commitment, the layer-zero,
C2, and three fold roots, the grinding nonces, and the selector.  No
probabilistic polynomial-time prover produces two distinct accepting proofs
with these fixed values, except with probability at most
\(\varepsilon_{\rm cr}+\varepsilon_{\rm round}^{(0)}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The roots are absorbed before their dependent challenges, so all committed
codewords and masks are fixed before any challenge is drawn; each private leaf
is \(\mathrm{SHA256}(\mathtt{0x10}\,\|\,\text{tag}\,\|\,\text{value}\,\|\,
\text{salt})\), so its salt is inside the pre-image bound by the root and
cannot be re-randomized.  Fixing \(x\), all roots, nonces, and selector fixes
the honest field values that the committed vectors determine: each
out-of-domain, sumcheck, fold, and \(D\)-lane claim has one value consistent
with the committed low-degree extension.  Any accepting proof whose field
claims deviate from those honest values passes the query-phase consistency
check of Theorem~\ref{thm:bcs-soundness} only with probability at most
\(\varepsilon_{\rm round}^{(0)}\), since low-degreeness is enforced only by
that phase; so, except on that event, both accepting proofs carry the honest
field claims, hence identical challenges and identical query positions.  Two
proofs that agree on all field claims and positions but differ must differ in
an opened Merkle path: a pair of distinct openings authenticating to the same
root at the same position is a Merkle second preimage, of probability at most
\(\varepsilon_{\rm cr}\).  The union bounds the event.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional simulation-extractability]
\label{thm:sim-extractability}
Assume Assumption~\ref{ass:sr-knowledge}, so that the compiled argument is a
proof of knowledge in the random-oracle model
(Theorem~\ref{thm:argument-of-knowledge}), and SHA-256 as a random oracle.  Let
\(A\) be a probabilistic polynomial-time adversary that receives simulated
proofs for valid statements, produced by the simulator of
Theorem~\ref{thm:real-vs-sim23}, and outputs a fresh accepting pair
\((x^\ast,\pi^\ast)\) --- accepting, and not one of the received proofs --- while
the experiment makes \(T\le2^{128}\) oracle queries and \(A\) receives
\(q_S\) simulated proofs.  Then there is an extractor that, given \(A\)'s
query--answer transcript, outputs \(w^\ast\) with
\(\RProfile(x^\ast,w^\ast)=1\), with work-normalized knowledge error \(3b(T)\)
and additional raw error at most
\[
 \varepsilon_{\rm cr}+\varepsilon_{\rm round}^{(0)}
 \;+\;
 \frac{R\,q_S\,(T+R\,q_S)}{2^{256}},
 \qquad R=32 .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The argument is public-coin, is a proof of knowledge in the random-oracle model
by Theorem~\ref{thm:argument-of-knowledge}, and has weak unique responses by
Lemma~\ref{lem:quasi-unique-response}.  We give the non-malleability reduction
explicitly, following the Fiat--Shamir template of
\citet{FaustKohlweissMarsonVenturi2012} adapted to the multi-round
random-oracle straight-line setting.  The reduction runs \(A\), answering each
simulation query by sampling the challenges first and programming the oracle at
that proof's \(R\) transcript prefixes, and it invokes the proof-of-knowledge
extractor of Theorem~\ref{thm:argument-of-knowledge} on the final proof.  That
extractor is applied against the oracle \emph{as seen through \(A\)'s own
queries}: statement-first absorption makes every challenge-defining prefix of
\(\pi^\ast\) a function of \(x^\ast\), so a prefix of \(\pi^\ast\) coincides
with one of the at most \(Rq_S\) programmed prefixes only by a birthday
collision against \(A\)'s \(T\) queries, bounded by \(Rq_S(T+Rq_S)/2^{256}\).
When \(x^\ast\) equals a simulated statement, an early prefix may instead
coincide deterministically; but the simulated roots are witness-free, and the
fresh grinding nonce and selector (absorbed last) shift the query positions, so
opening a reused witness-free root at those positions passes the query phase
only with probability \(\varepsilon_{\rm round}^{(0)}\).  By weak unique
response (Lemma~\ref{lem:quasi-unique-response}), reusing a programmed prefix
without recomputing its opening is a second preimage or a query-phase
violation, already inside
\(\varepsilon_{\rm cr}+\varepsilon_{\rm round}^{(0)}\).  Outside these events
every challenge-defining and opening value of \(\pi^\ast\) is answered by
\(A\)'s own queries, so \(\pi^\ast\) is a plain-oracle proof to which the
extractor of Theorem~\ref{thm:argument-of-knowledge} applies, yielding
\(w^\ast\) with its work-normalized knowledge error \(3b(T)\).  The collision
and second-preimage terms are raw, of the order already present in the
soundness ledger; \(3b(T)\) is the work-normalized knowledge floor.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Deployed-pool theft resistance]
\label{cor:deployed-theft}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:sr-knowledge} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:quasi-unique-response}, no efficient party lacking a note's
secret \(k_\nu\) produces an accepting spend of it, even after observing any
number \(q_H\) of honest spend proofs, with work-normalized knowledge error
\(3b(T)\) and additional raw error at most
\(\varepsilon_{\rm cr}^{\rm SHA}+\varepsilon_{\rm cr}^{\rm Pos}
+\varepsilon_{\rm round}^{(0)}+Rq_H(T+Rq_H)/2^{256}+q_H\,\varepsilon_{\rm RS}\),
summing the SHA-256 Merkle second-preimage event inherited through
Theorem~\ref{thm:sim-extractability} and the Poseidon2 nullifier-binding event
inherited through Corollary~\ref{cor:theft-resistance}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Honest spends are proofs of valid statements for distinct notes.  Replacing the
\(q_H\) honest proofs one at a time with simulated proofs costs
\(q_H\varepsilon_{\rm RS}\) by the zero-knowledge bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:real-vs-sim23} (\(q_S=q_H\)).  In the resulting experiment
Theorem~\ref{thm:sim-extractability} extracts a witness from \(A\)'s accepting
spend for the statement carrying the victim nullifier \(\nu\); the
second-preimage argument of Corollary~\ref{cor:theft-resistance} forces its
secret to be \(k_\nu\).  In the theft-relevant case the victim has not yet
spent \(\nu\), so no honest or simulated proof for \(x^\ast\) exists and the
freshness condition holds trivially; a later second spend of \(\nu\) is refused
on-chain by the nullifier marker regardless.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope]
\label{rem:sim-extractability}
The result is classical and conditional on the same premises as the knowledge
theorem; it makes no quantum-random-oracle claim.  The non-malleability step is
the explicit straight-line reduction above, adapting the Fiat--Shamir template
of \citet{FaustKohlweissMarsonVenturi2012} rather than invoking a
multi-round or algebraic-group-model theorem as a black box; the simulator is a
proof device, and is strictly stronger than a deployment adversary, which
cannot program SHA-256.  The guarantee concerns the production
of accepting proofs only, and does not address key management, secret-key
leakage, or the local and network observables excluded from the declared view
of Definition~\ref{def:spend-complete-view}.
\end{remark}
