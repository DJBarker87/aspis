\section{Security experiment and soundness}
\label{sec:soundness}

\subsection{Threat model}

The adversary chooses a public statement and proof after observing the public
history.  It succeeds when the verifier accepts but the statement has no
witness satisfying \cref{def:spend}.  The adversary may adapt its oracle
queries and later messages to the transcript prefix already available.  Its
resources include SHA-256 oracle queries and the work spent searching for the
six nonces.

All failure events are defined in one finite probability space
\((\Omega,\mu)\).  Let \(A_{\mathrm{false}}\subseteq\Omega\) be the event of
accepting a false statement.  Let \(C\) be the protocol failure event, which
combines the low-degree, query, relation, and work terms.  The remaining
events describe either a deterministic mismatch between the deployed
execution and the formal experiment or a cryptographic failure such as a hash
collision.

This single-experiment formulation matters because event bounds taken from
different conditioned experiments cannot safely be added.  In Lean, the
coverage theorem is pointwise:
\begin{equation}
  A_{\mathrm{false}}
  \subseteq C\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{18}E_i.
  \label{eq:event-coverage}
\end{equation}
The detailed protocol ledger has six entries: the query and final-work event,
four fold events, and relation repair.  Lean proves that their union equals
\(C\).
The eighteen external events are grouped in \cref{tab:failure-interface}.

deterministic interfaces are recorded so that proofs can make their events
empty instead of assigning them arbitrary small probabilities.

\subsection{Symbolic decomposition}

Taking measures in \cref{eq:event-coverage} and applying the union bound gives
\begin{equation}
  \Prob[A_{\mathrm{false}}]
  \leq \Prob[C]+\sum_{i=1}^{18}\Prob[E_i].
  \label{eq:symbolic-union}
\end{equation}
Lean proves this inequality over the exact ordered event list and proves that
each event appears once.  No independence assumption is used.

For the conventional cryptographic presentation, let
\(D_{\mathrm{prod}}\) assert all deterministic implementation, compilation,
and runtime correspondences.  Under \(D_{\mathrm{prod}}\), those mismatch
events are empty.  The remaining six probabilistic events are the five
primitive events and credential recovery; writing them as
\(P_1,\ldots,P_6\) yields
\begin{equation}
  \Prob[A_{\mathrm{false}}]
  \leq \Prob[C]+\sum_{j=1}^{6}\Prob[P_j]
  \qquad\text{under }D_{\mathrm{prod}}.
  \label{eq:deterministic-probabilistic-split}
\end{equation}
This split preserves the larger audit ledger while giving deterministic
claims their natural logical role.

\subsection{Bounded distinct-query sampling}

The query sampler is modeled exactly, including its sixty-four-draw limit and
first-occurrence rule.  Conditioned on collecting eighteen positions, every
ordered list of eighteen distinct elements has the same number of preimages.
For a fixed bad set of size at most \(A\) in a domain of size \(N\), the
probability that all queries land in that set is therefore
\begin{equation}
  \prod_{j=0}^{17}\frac{A-j}{N-j}.
  \label{eq:without-replacement}
\end{equation}
The same expression upper-bounds the unconditioned event that sampling
succeeds and every output is bad, because exhaustion causes rejection.  The
concrete calculation uses
\[
  N=131{,}072,
  \qquad A\leq6{,}082.
\]
Lean proves both uniformity and the resulting finite rational inequality.

\subsection{Low-degree and relation reductions}

The low-degree proof has three layers.  First, the formalization checks that
the concrete encoders meet the field, ordering, degree, distance, agreement,
and multiplicity hypotheses of the cited circle-code decoding result.  The
general decoding theorem remains a literature assumption.

Second, \cref{thm:four-fold-extraction} preserves one decoder candidate through
all four folds.  Each exceptional challenge set is determined by the
appropriate transcript prefix.  A separate adaptive-oracle theorem shows
that \(Q\) completed fresh attempts cost at most \(Q\) times the one-attempt
bound, even when the next attempt depends on the full previous history.

Third, the algebraic relation connects the extracted candidate to
\cref{def:spend}.  Nineteen columns are batched with a nonzero extension-field
challenge of maximum exponent eighteen.  Sequential challenges bound the
tuples capable of repairing false scalar claims, while the fold-duality proof
keeps candidate values and verifier weights consistent.  The final semantic
step is \cref{thm:spend-after-extraction}.

The Fiat--Shamir step uses the published state-restoration analysis for
interactive oracle proofs \cite{BenSassonChiesaSpooner2016,
BlockGarretaKatzThalerTiwariZajac2023,CarmonEtAl2026Stwo}.  The byte-level
transcript proof determines the exact oracle inputs and causal schedule.  The
random-oracle replacement and applicability of the published theorem are
explicit assumptions at this boundary.

\subsection{Work-normalized accounting}

The six proof-of-work checks restrict the fraction of transcript attempts that
reach their protected challenges.  In a work-normalized experiment, repeated
trials are charged to the adversary.  With nineteen columns, maximum batching
exponent eighteen, thirty public-coin boundaries, and the work difficulties
in \cref{tab:profile-parameters}, Lean verifies the concrete rational
arithmetic and proves
\begin{equation}
  B_{\mathrm{protocol}}
  \leq \frac{7}{10\cdot2^{100}}.
  \label{eq:core-budget}
\end{equation}
This inequality is conditional on the checked decoding applicability,
code-membership connection, authenticated openings, Fiat--Shamir theorem,
and grinding-output model described above.

Let \(B_{\mathrm{external}}\) bound the six probabilistic events remaining
under \(D_{\mathrm{prod}}\), and assume
\begin{equation}
  B_{\mathrm{external}}
  \leq \frac{3}{10\cdot2^{100}}.
  \label{eq:external-budget}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional work-normalized soundness]
\label{thm:conditional-soundness}
Assume the event coverage of \cref{eq:event-coverage}; the checked connection
from the exact protocol event to the concrete arithmetic; the published
decoding and Fiat--Shamir results with their verified parameter hypotheses;
the deterministic correspondences \(D_{\mathrm{prod}}\); and primitive and
credential bounds satisfying \cref{eq:external-budget}.  Then
\[
  \Prob[A_{\mathrm{false}}]\leq2^{-100}.
\]
\end{theorem}

The final step is ordinary arithmetic:
\[
  \Prob[A_{\mathrm{false}}]
  \leq B_{\mathrm{protocol}}+B_{\mathrm{external}}
  \leq 0.7\cdot2^{-100}+0.3\cdot2^{-100}
  =2^{-100}.
\]
The substantive checked content is the preceding pointwise classification,
event-ledger equality, parameter application, and protocol arithmetic.

\subsection{Raw resource-bounded accounting}

Work-normalized probability and probability after a completed grind answer
different questions.  Let \(1\leq Q\leq2^{128}\) be the adversary's SHA-256
oracle-query budget, let \(R\leq32\) bound the public-coin boundaries, and let
\(\varepsilon_{\mathrm I}\) bound each interactive protocol branch.  The
state-restoration specialization represented in Lean is
\[
  b(Q)=\left(1+\frac{R}{Q}\right)\varepsilon_{\mathrm I}
       +3\left(Q+\frac1Q\right)2^{-256}.
\]
Its corresponding resource-bounded form is
\begin{align}
  \operatorname{Adv}^{\mathrm{false}}_{\Aspis}(\mathcal A;Q)
  \leq {}&3(Q+R)\varepsilon_{\mathrm I}
       +9(Q^2+1)2^{-256} \notag\\
       &+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{prim}}(\mathcal A,Q)
       +\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cred}}(\mathcal A),
  \label{eq:resource-bounded-advantage}
\end{align}
under \(D_{\mathrm{prod}}\) and the same decoding and Fiat--Shamir
assumptions.  The two final functions collect the primitive and credential
advantages and are left symbolic.

Once the 37-bit initial work search has been completed, its factor cannot also
be charged in the exponent.  Removing that normalization leaves the dominant
explicit term at roughly 70--71 bits, plus the displayed primitive,
credential, and runtime terms.  We report this raw view alongside
\cref{thm:conditional-soundness} so the unit of every security figure remains
clear.
