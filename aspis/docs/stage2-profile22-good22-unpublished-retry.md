# Profile 22 Good22 unpublished-retry theorem

Date: 2026-07-13

Status: **conditional privacy theorem, verifier-neutral host gate, bounded
unpublished production attempt manager, and fixed-boundary public-channel
controller; not a liveness claim.**  The exact affine containment implication
is enforced on every schedule accepted by the host gate.  A uniform
quantitative bound on rejection probability is still missing, so the fixed
cap has no proved availability error.  The checked-in controller fixes the
declared channel shape; integrating its private worker/public timer events is
the wallet's responsibility.

## Ruling

First-good unpublished rejection sampling is a valid privacy construction,
but it closes a different statement from the one an ordinary rank replay
closes.

For privacy of the complete capped public view, a numerical bad-schedule
bound is **not** intrinsically required: it is enough that the joint candidate
view and the gate-success law are witness-independent.  The retry index and
the final no-transaction symbol then have the same distribution for every
valid witness.

A numerical bound **is required** for each of the following claims:

1. the honest prover produces a proof with useful probability;
2. the capped no-proof probability is at most `2^-100` (or `2^-128`);
3. the expected proving/mining time is small; and
4. the selected cap `A <= 16` is an adequate operational policy rather than
   merely an upper bound used in the EPRO privacy ledger.

Profile 22 does not yet have that bound.  The three candidate degree ledgers
remain conditional on an every-q nonzero-polynomial or structural theorem.
The fact that one frozen schedule is green cannot justify retries.

## Exact schedule-only predicate

Fix a public atomic statement `x`, the profile-22 wire/layout fingerprint,
and a parser-valid public verifier schedule `s`.  Let `F=M31`.  Let `K_mask`
be the complete frozen mask-coin vector and let

```text
A_x,s : K_mask -> V_x,s
```

be the canonical M31-linear map to every non-hash public field coordinate,
after quotienting only explicit public identities.  The view includes raw
C1/C2 openings, all masked zerocheck messages, three terminal points, the
gamma-combined root word, later folds, OOD/relation values, and final
coefficients.  Merkle roots/frontiers and transcript hashes are handled by
the EPRO/private-Merkle hybrid rather than inserted as fake linear rows.

Let `D_phys,x,s` be generated by exactly:

- each semantic column's physical `B_c`: active `e_r`, inactive
  `e_r-e_d_c`, fixed row zero excluded, and dependent `d_c` excluded;
- every legal zero-initial-claim degree-27 sumcheck difference; and
- every active zero-sum helper difference.

The minimal predicate is

```text
Good22_min(x,s) iff
rank_F(A_x,s) = rank_F([A_x,s | D_phys,x,s]).                 (1)
```

The matrix order, quotient relations and source basis are canonical.  The
predicate may use only `x`, the frozen definition fingerprint, and public
verifier coins/OOD/fold/query data in `s`.  It must not inspect witness
values, realized field masks, leaf salts, roots, opened values, proof length,
retry state, or a prover-selected minor.

Concretely, the gate reads only the fresh-RO-derived
`lambda,chi,theta,mu,eta,z,gamma`, circle/line OOD points and mixes, fold
`alpha`, and the distinct q16 indices (plus frozen public shape data).  It
does not read any absorbed sumcheck coefficient, terminal evaluation, root,
opening or witness value.  In EPRO those fresh answers have a common
witness-independent law up to the already-booked pre-query/collision event.
This gives a common success/abort/retry-count law without first proving a
numerical `beta`; the numerical bound remains purely a liveness obligation.

The checked-in host function

```text
evaluate_profile22_strong_good_schedule(s)
```

implements a stronger predicate.  It first requires the canonical raw and
sumcheck quotient builders to reach their pinned ranks, proves that the
post-raw mask image is the exact kernel of the compressed verifier-terminal
functional, then checks protocol-coupled physical, zero-initial/zero-terminal
legal-sumcheck and helper containment exactly.  The deliberately overstrong
independent-terminal direct sum is retained only as a red negative tooth. Thus

```text
Good22_strong(x,s) => Good22_min(x,s),                       (2)
```

but the converse need not hold.  In particular, the strong gate may reject a
Boolean-`z` schedule on which both mask and target raw images collapse while
minimal containment still holds.  This only hurts liveness; it does not
weaken privacy.  Shape/layout/encoder differential failures are fatal local
errors and must not be converted into retryable schedules.

The gate is host-only.  It is called only by the profile-22 first-good attempt
manager after a complete candidate is built; it is not called by the verifier
or the fixture-only raw one-attempt entry point.
Its frozen definition fingerprint is

```text
59ea6e29e7231a4346d94035afc6213b2b4e8f57fb505615b6f54119e1ef839b.
```

Changing profile, rate, q, factor schedule, layout, source basis, quotient,
PCS schedule or rank field requires a new fingerprint and theorem artifact.

## Attempt experiment

For one fixed statement `x` and valid witness `w`, attempt `i` samples three
fresh independent nonzero 256-bit values:

```text
nu_i       public attempt nonce,
sigma_i    private field-mask seed,
tau_i      private leaf-salt seed.
```

Before either private seed is used, `nu_i` is durably appended to the
wallet-global burn ledger.  Profile 22 binds the statement digest, atomic
mask/layout fingerprints, profile-22 tree descriptor and `nu_i` into every
field/salt/transcript domain.  The attempt is then built and all six work
records are canonically mined locally.  Only then is the final q16 schedule
known and `Good22_strong(x,s_i)` evaluated.

Attempts use independent secrets and disjoint random-oracle prefixes.  In the
EPRO proof this independence is conditioned on the already-booked pre-query
and collision complement.  Mere nonce uniqueness is insufficient: `nu_i`
must remain unpredictable before the adversary's pre-proof queries, and
`sigma_i,tau_i` must be independent rather than derived from it or each
other.

On a bad schedule, the returned candidate's proof/opening buffers, roots,
schedule and relation trace are scrubbed before drop.  Private attempt seeds
already use zeroizing wrappers.  The burn record is retained.  A new attempt
starts with a new triple.  No failed root, nonce, proof byte, proof-account
chunk, log, telemetry event, network message or remote-miner request may be
published.  The unpublished miner suppresses progress stderr and creates no
checkpoint.  Extending explicit zeroization to every large temporary
encoder/Merkle allocation remains memory-hardening, not a public-view
prerequisite.

The production entry point accepts the concrete filesystem-backed durable
nonce store, not the in-memory test implementation, and fixes the transcript
hash to the canonical host SHA-256 implementation rather than accepting a
caller-selected hash function.  Controlled entropy, build, PoW-process and
gate errors collapse to the same public failure type.
Rust invariant panics or process aborts remain implementation faults outside
that returned-error boundary; claiming a fixed external failure shape still
requires eliminating or containing them in the wallet process.

The bundled quiet CPU miner computes the global-minimum valid nonce.  If
`ASPIS_POW_MINER` is configured, that external executable is part of the
trusted honest prover and **must** implement the same global-minimum rule.  The
host rechecks the consensus validity predicate, but validity alone cannot
certify that an external answer is the minimum.

## Precise first-good theorem

The convenient fixed-schedule translation argument is not sufficient by
itself because the Fiat--Shamir schedule is derived from mask-dependent
commitments.  The required hypothesis is joint, not pointwise.

For every public history `h` of earlier unpublished attempts, suppose there
exists a witness-independent one-attempt distribution `Sim_x,h` such that,
for every valid witness `w`:

1. the joint real distribution of `(S,Y,H,G)` is `epsilon_att`-close to
   `Sim_x,h`; here `S` is the complete public schedule, `Y` the affine field
   view, `H` the hash/Merkle/work wrapper, and
   `G=Good22_strong(x,S)`;
2. on every `G=1` schedule, Equation (1) holds, so the conditional affine
   view has the exact mask-translation simulator;
3. the simulator includes the salted Merkle roots/openings/frontiers, exact
   canonical-minimum work-nonce law and all adaptive post-proof RO queries;
4. candidate-attempt domains are fresh and the per-history success law is
   the same for all witnesses; and
5. all non-proof public behavior is a deterministic function of `x`, the
   simulated candidate, and an explicitly simulated release channel.

Run at most `T` attempts, emit the first good candidate, and otherwise emit a
single fixed abort symbol `bot`.  Then the complete public output, **including
the abort symbol and any explicitly included retry index**, is at most

```text
T * epsilon_att + epsilon_side                            (3)
```

apart between any two valid witnesses (with the usual separately booked EPRO
programming/collision terms).  If the per-attempt simulation is exact and
attempts are i.i.d. with bad probability `beta`, then

```text
Pr[J=j]   = beta^(j-1) (1-beta),  1 <= j <= T,
Pr[bot]   = beta^T,
Pr[S in E, J=j] = beta^(j-1) Pr[S in E and Good22(S)].      (4)
```

Thus `J`, the accepted schedule and `bot` have a witness-independent joint
law.  There is no additive `beta` privacy term.  If one reports privacy only
conditional on success, the approximate bound in (3) must additionally be
divided by a proved lower bound on `1-beta^T`; keeping `bot` in the public
view avoids hiding that conditioning loss.

The all-schedule affine rank implication (2) is only one hypothesis of this
theorem.  It does not prove that the schedule distribution is common after
fixing mask-dependent roots.  That step is supplied by the EPRO programming
hybrid; otherwise the standard counterexample `S=R, Y=w+R` defeats the claim
despite full rank from `R` to `Y`.

## Retry count and timing

Retry count may be mathematically public under Equation (4), but the selected
engineering policy keeps it private because real wall-clock behavior is not
just the integer `J`.

The following are part of the privacy view if observable:

- per-attempt rank time and memory pressure;
- field rejection-sampler counts;
- six canonical work-search minima and GPU/CPU timing;
- crashes, thermal throttling and scheduler delays;
- nonce-ledger/backup growth;
- progress UI, logs and telemetry;
- proof-account upload start time; and
- remote prover/miner traffic.

The safe release policy is a statement-dependent but witness-independent
public epoch.  The wallet buffers the first good proof and releases it only at
that epoch; if no proof is ready after `T` attempts, it releases the same
public no-transaction outcome.  Internal attempts produce no externally
visible events.  Alternatively, every timing/channel distribution must be
included in `epsilon_side` and simulated.  A fixed retry cap without a fixed
release policy does not hide timing.

The checked-in `Profile22FixedReleaseController` implements that public edge
as an injectable-clock state machine.  It is constructed before the private
first-good worker starts.  A completion observed strictly before the selected
boundary is buffered locally; no completion, a controlled error, an unmined
candidate, or a completion observed at/after the boundary all select the same
payload-free `Abort`.  The boundary timer wins an exact timestamp tie.  At the
boundary, `release` consumes the controller and invokes
`Profile22ReleaseChannel::publish` exactly once with either `Proof(bytes)` or
`Abort`.  No other method invokes the channel, and the opaque return token
carries no branch bit.

The production first-good entry point returns an opaque
`Profile22FirstGoodCandidate`, not the underlying proof structure.  Its fields
are private, it can be constructed only after the mined Good22 manager
accepts, the fixed-release controller is its only publication consumer, and
dropping it scrubs the candidate.  This seals the normal API path against
accidentally serializing the worker result before the public boundary.

The controller does not spawn, log, or monitor attempts.  The wallet event
loop must keep the first-good worker and durable nonce ledger private, deliver
worker completion to `record_first_good_completion`, and invoke `release` at
the configured public-boundary event.  The included `SystemTime` clock waits
in short chunks; an injected logical clock makes the boundary rule exactly
testable.  The clock prevents an early release, but it cannot repair a late
wallet callback: a late call publishes late and its delay belongs in
`epsilon_side`.  This is a public-channel state machine, not a claim of
protection against local hardware, scheduler, filesystem, power, process, or
OS wake-up jitter side channels.

Because one profile-22 attempt performs expected six-stage work of about
`1.136e12` SHA predicates, a literal worst-case sixteen-attempt release window
is not an acceptable operational assumption.  A proven tiny `beta` is needed
before selecting a realistic window even though it is not logically required
for witness indistinguishability.

Local access to the burned-nonce ledger is outside the current public Solana
view.  If wallet storage, cloud backup or telemetry is in the adversary view,
its entry count exposes `J` and must be covered by Equation (4) and the side-
channel model.  A rejected nonce file is never deleted.

## Quantitative cap ruling

The selected computational-ROM ledger uses `A<=16` and
`C=961,765`, giving the conditional leading term

```text
A*C*2^128 / 2^256 = 2^-104.1246751001.
```

That number bounds privacy programming for at most sixteen attempts; it does
not prove that a good attempt occurs.  No extra factor of sixteen should be
added for a retry count after it has already been included as `A`, but every
additional hidden RO family must still enter `C`.

The current candidate liveness rows are:

| assumed closure | per-attempt bound | cap for `2^-100` no-proof | usable now |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| structural root-neutral lemma, raw only | `3240/(2^31-1)` | 6 | no: lemma open |
| every-q root-neutral sumcheck minor | `41040/(2^31-1)` | 7 | no |
| every-q full residual PCS minor | `6085920/(2^31-1)` | 12 | no |

Cap 16 is therefore a privacy-accounting ceiling only.  Promoting it to a
production liveness claim requires one of those every-q premises, plus a
fixed-release implementation and measurement.

## Privacy, liveness and soundness are separate

**Privacy.**  On accepted schedules, canonical containment plus the EPRO
Merkle/transcript hybrid simulates the proof.  Unpublished first-good
selection adds no `beta` term when its complete success law is common.

**Liveness/completeness.**  A cap `T` loses `beta^T` probability only after a
uniform `beta` theorem.  There is currently no usable number.  A green frozen
attempt is regression evidence, not a lower bound on success probability.

**Soundness.**  The gate is honest-prover-only and the verifier/wire are
unchanged, so the accepted language and verifier acceptance predicate are
unchanged.  The gate neither improves nor weakens the 102.4647-bit Johnson
ledger.  Malicious-prover Fiat--Shamir grinding remains covered by the
declared adversarial RO-query bound; honest rejection must not be booked as a
fresh-challenge soundness event.  If rank failure is ever found to weaken a
binding check rather than privacy alone, the verifier must enforce it or the
event must return to the soundness ledger.

## Promotion gates

1. Independently replay the strong gate on the final profile-22 fixture and
   pin the exact ranks/fingerprint.
2. Prove an every-q structural or nonzero-polynomial theorem for the exact
   selected predicate and derive a uniform `beta`. Fixed-schedule containment
   is now closed: full raw-plus-masked-sumcheck rank `1080`, the executable
   terminal-kernel identity, and the coupled replay imply physical/legal PCS
   containment. What remains is proving that the required raw-plus-sumcheck
   rank is reached with the claimed probability over every distinct q16
   schedule (or replacing that premise with a proved joint q/z bound).
3. Audit/extend best-effort scrubbing to all large temporary allocations; the
   returned rejected candidate and private seeds are already scrubbed.
4. **Closed for the declared channel model:** the injectable-clock
   fixed-release controller and tests show that attempts `1..T`, exhaustion,
   controlled errors and late completion expose exactly one boundary-time
   proof/abort channel event.  Local hardware/OS observables remain outside
   that model.
5. Test durable distinct burns across rejection, crash/reopen and concurrent
   wallet processes; never erase a failed burn.
6. Pin a production-view test with no failed root, nonce, proof chunk, work
   checkpoint, retry count, log or return data.
7. Recompute the EPRO inventory for the final attempt manager and release
   channel, mine the accepted proof locally, and run the production atomic
   mutation path.

Until the remaining gates close, the correct claim is: profile 22 has an
exact, schedule-only, verifier-neutral strong containment gate, a hard-capped
unpublished attempt manager, an injectable-clock fixed-boundary public-channel
controller, and a rigorous conditional first-good privacy theorem.  It does
not have a quantitative availability theorem or a local-hardware
side-channel theorem.
