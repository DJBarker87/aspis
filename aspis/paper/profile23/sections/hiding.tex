\section{Complete-view computational hiding}
\label{sec:hiding}

The hiding statement compares witnesses for one fixed valid public statement.
It includes the proof's variable-length serialization and the public abort
event; it does not condition on successful proving.

\subsection{Declared view and games}

\begin{definition}[Complete declared view]
\label{def:profile23-complete-view}
For public input \(x\), the declared output is either
\[
 \mathsf{Proof}(V)\qquad\text{or}\qquad \mathsf{Abort}.
\]
The value \(V\) contains the complete finalized proof-account bytes, all
commitment roots, opened \(\mathsf{value}\|\mathsf{salt}\) records and
minimal-subtree frontiers, Fiat--Shamir and work nonces, the branch selector,
proof length, program-visible application-instruction framing and logs, and
the deterministic public pool and nullifier transition.  It does not include
the fee-payer signature or identity, recent blockhash, transaction origin,
account-graph linkage outside the statement, or network metadata.  The proof
length is allowed to depend on the public query schedule.  The output occurs
at one fixed public release boundary.
\end{definition}

The cryptographic games below do not use elapsed prover time as a random
variable.  They assume that each permitted attempt completes before the
release boundary.  Equivalently, they may be composed with an external cutoff
flag that is independent of the witness, masks, oracle answers, attempt state,
and private execution time: the same flag is coupled in both games, and a set
flag maps either output to \(\mathsf{Abort}\).  The internal cap-exhaustion
event and this external truncation are analytically distinct even though they
have the same public encoding.

Let \(\mathcal H:\{0,1\}^*\rightarrow\{0,1\}^{256}\) be one programmable
random oracle.  Rigid domain encodings separate its transcript, field
expansion, salt expansion, Merkle, statement, and work uses.  In the real
experiment \(\mathsf{Real}_{23}^{\mathcal H}(x,w)\), the honest prover uses
fresh attempt secrets, chooses the least good of three schedules, retries at
most 17 completed attempts, and emits
Definition~\ref{def:profile23-complete-view}.  The
simulator experiment \(\mathsf{Sim}_{23}^{\mathcal H}(x)\) has no witness and
may program \(\mathcal H\) at fresh inputs.

A classical distinguisher receives arbitrary auxiliary input, observes the
output, and may make adaptive oracle queries both before and after that
output.  Its total budget is \(Q_H\le2^{128}\).  Define
\begin{align*}
 \epsilon_{\rm RS}
   &=\sup_{x,w,\mathcal D}
       \left|\Pr[\mathcal D^{\mathcal H}
          (\mathsf{Real}_{23}^{\mathcal H}(x,w))=1]
       -\Pr[\mathcal D^{\mathcal H}
          (\mathsf{Sim}_{23}^{\mathcal H}(x))=1]\right|,\\
 \epsilon_{\rm pair}
   &=\sup_{x,w_0,w_1,\mathcal D}
       \left|\Pr[\mathcal D^{\mathcal H}
          (\mathsf{Real}_{23}^{\mathcal H}(x,w_0))=1]
       -\Pr[\mathcal D^{\mathcal H}
          (\mathsf{Real}_{23}^{\mathcal H}(x,w_1))=1]\right|,
\end{align*}
where each quantified witness satisfies \(R_{23}(x,w)=1\).
The programming experiment follows the explicitly programmable random-oracle
(EPRO) compilation framework of
Ben-Sasson, Chiesa, and Spooner~\cite{BenSassonChiesaSpooner2016}, specialized
below to the concrete Profile~23 transcript and private-Merkle wire.

\subsection{Affine field view}

Split every \(\mathbb K\) element into four \(\mathbb F\) coordinates.  Fix
a public schedule \(s_{\rm fs}\), and quotient the verifier's exact public
linear identities.  The complete non-hash field view of an honest prover has
the form
\begin{equation}
  Y_{s_{\rm fs}}(w,R)=A_{s_{\rm fs}}R+b_{s_{\rm fs}}(w),
  \label{eq:affine-field-view}
\end{equation}
where \(R\in\mathbb F^m\) consists of ideal independent mask coordinates and
\(A_{s_{\rm fs}}\) is a public matrix determined by the frozen layout and
schedule.  Let the public verifier identities be
\[
 L_{x,s_{\rm fs}}y=c_{x,s_{\rm fs}},\qquad
 \mathcal S_{x,s_{\rm fs}}
   =\{y:L_{x,s_{\rm fs}}y=c_{x,s_{\rm fs}}\},
 \quad
 \mathcal W_{s_{\rm fs}}=\ker L_{x,s_{\rm fs}}.
\]
The homogeneous space is independent of the witness and, after public values
are moved to the right-hand side, of \(x\).

\begin{lemma}[Uniform image and constant fibers]
\label{lem:uniform-mask-preimages}
Let \(A:\mathbb F^m\rightarrow\mathbb F^n\) be linear, with rank \(r\) and
image \(V\).  Every \(v\in V\) has exactly
\(
 |\ker A|=p^{m-r}
\)
preimages.  Consequently, if \(R\) is uniform in \(\mathbb F^m\), then
\(AR\) is uniform in \(V\).  If, in addition,
\(b(w_1)-b(w_0)\in V\), then
\(AR+b(w_0)\) and \(AR+b(w_1)\) are identically distributed.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For any \(v\in V\), choose \(r_v\) with \(Ar_v=v\).  The full preimage is
the coset \(r_v+\ker A\), hence has \(p^{m-r}\) elements by rank--nullity.
It follows that
\(
 \Pr[AR=v]=p^{m-r}/p^m=p^{-r}
\)
for each of the \(p^r\) elements of \(V\).  Finally,
\(b(w_1)-b(w_0)\in V\) implies
\(b(w_0)+V=b(w_1)+V\), so both translated uniform distributions have the
same support and mass function.
\end{proof}

\begin{assumption}[Complete affine-image and rank coverage]
\label{lem:complete-affine-image}
For the frozen Profile~23 prover, verifier, layout, and serialization, let
\(Y_{s_{\rm fs}}\) include every non-hash field coordinate in the declared
view.  For every valid \((x,w)\) and every schedule accepted by
\(\mathsf{Good}_{23}\), Equation~\eqref{eq:affine-field-view} is an exact
representation of that complete vector, with independent uniform mask
coordinates \(R\) and no omitted witness-dependent coordinate.  Moreover, the
production matrices have all eight ranks in Table~\ref{tab:good23-ranks},
satisfy their source guards, and their semantic, \(G/D\), inactive-balanced
\(H_1\), terminal, and root-neutral blocks cover the full homogeneous
verifier space.  Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{im}A_{s_{\rm fs}}=\mathcal W_{s_{\rm fs}},
 \qquad
 \mathcal S_{x,s_{\rm fs}}
 =y_{x,s_{\rm fs}}+\operatorname{im}A_{s_{\rm fs}}
 \label{eq:image-equality}
\end{equation}
for a canonical public origin \(y_{x,s_{\rm fs}}\) computable by row
reduction.  In particular, for any two valid witnesses for \(x\),
\[
b_{s_{\rm fs}}(w_1)-b_{s_{\rm fs}}(w_0)
   \in\operatorname{im}A_{s_{\rm fs}}.
\]
Writing \(r_s=\dim\mathcal W_{s_{\rm fs}}\), the equality also gives
\(\operatorname{rank}A_{s_{\rm fs}}=r_s\) and
\(|\ker A_{s_{\rm fs}}|=p^{m-r_s}\).
\end{assumption}

The release supplies executable evidence for this premise.  Every
reconstructed mask direction satisfies the homogeneous public identities, so
\(\operatorname{im}A_{s_{\rm fs}}\subseteq\mathcal W_{s_{\rm fs}}\).
For the reverse-coverage check, it decomposes a homogeneous field view into
(i) the separately authenticated semantic raw coordinates, (ii) the physical
\(H_1,G,D\) raw coordinates and their three terminal values, and (iii) the
masked-sumcheck coordinates after fixing the initial and terminal claims.

The structural semantic maps cover every queried raw semantic coordinate;
their balanced kernels and lane-separated terminal projections cover the
remaining semantic terminals.  The two 288-rank query maps and 12-rank Schur
complements in Table~\ref{tab:good23-ranks} cover the remaining \(G/D\) and
inactive-balanced \(H_1\) raw coordinates.  After these raw values are fixed,
the remaining sumcheck difference belongs to
\(\ker(\mathrm{initial},T_z)\).  The root-neutral product has rank 1404, of
which 328 coordinates cover the terminal-plus-initial projection and 1076
cover that kernel.  Hence every homogeneous solution is a sum of legal mask
directions in the reconstructed system, yielding
\(\mathcal W_{s_{\rm fs}}\subseteq\operatorname{im}A_{s_{\rm fs}}\).

The same decomposition gives a constructive translation.  Translate the
semantic one-time pads to match their raw view; use inactive-balanced
\(H_1\) padding for the physical \(H_1\) difference; pair \(D\) and source
changes with \(G\) so that
\(\Delta G=-\gamma\Delta D\) and the batched word is unchanged; then use the
1076 root-neutral directions to cancel the remaining sumcheck difference.
The combined message is now identically zero, so the linear encoder, OOD
maps, folds, later queried values, and final polynomial also agree.

Finally, row reduction of the public affine identities selects a canonical
solution by setting free coordinates to zero.  This computes
\(y_{x,s_{\rm fs}}\) without a witness and checks the second equality in
\eqref{eq:image-equality}.

The rank equalities used in this coverage argument are finite,
machine-checked statements over \(\mathbb F\).  The executable checker
reconstructs the matrices from
the frozen layout and public schedule, performs elimination, verifies target
dimensions, source guards, pivots, and nonzero minors, and rejects definition
drift.  The published component fingerprints record provenance; they are not
substituted for matrix reconstruction.  The certificate fixture supplies
frozen witnesses for the nonzero minors, while the production transfer uses
layout and \(\mathsf{Good}_{23}\)-definition fingerprints and a fresh replay
of all three
public schedules.  It does not assume that production proof bytes equal the
certificate fixture.

This evidence binds the premise to the release, but it is not an independent
verification of the completeness of the reconstructed affine model.  The
hiding theorems therefore remain conditional on
Assumption~\ref{lem:complete-affine-image} until such a verifier is published.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-mask-preimages} and
Assumption~\ref{lem:complete-affine-image}, uniform ideal masks give exactly the
uniform distribution on \(\mathcal S_{x,s_{\rm fs}}\).  The affine replacement
therefore has error \(\epsilon_{\rm aff}=0\) on every emitted schedule under
the premise.

\subsection{Witness-free simulator}

The simulator uses Equation~\eqref{eq:image-equality} directly.

\begin{description}
\item[\(\mathsf{Sim}_{23}(x)\).]
For each of at most 17 attempts, create a fresh common transcript state and
three label-44 branches using lazy random-oracle programming.  Evaluate the
public predicate \(\mathsf{Good}_{23}\) on all three schedules.  If none is
good, continue; if the cap is exhausted, output \(\mathsf{Abort}\).  Otherwise
select the least good schedule \(s_{\rm fs}\).  Reconstruct the public affine
system, compute its canonical origin \(y_{x,s_{\rm fs}}\), and row-reduce
\(A_{s_{\rm fs}}\) to a canonical basis
\(v_1,\ldots,v_r\) of its image.  Sample
\(c_1,\ldots,c_r\leftarrow\mathbb F\) and set
\[
 y=y_{x,s_{\rm fs}}+\sum_{j=1}^r c_j\,v_j.
\]
Use \(y\) as the complete non-hash field view.  Simulate the five salted
Merkle trees and their minimal frontiers, program the remaining transcript
states lazily, sample the six canonical-minimum work nonces with their exact
conditional laws, serialize the selected variable-length proof, finalize its
proof-account frame, and append the deterministic public execution view.
\end{description}

Every input to this algorithm is public.  In particular,
\(\mathsf{Good}_{23}\) consumes no witness, realized mask, private salt, or
retry-derived hidden value.

\begin{lemma}[Selection and cap-exhaustion law]
\label{lem:selection-hiding-abort}
Conditioned on no adversarial prequery of a programmed input and no
programmed-state collision, the three branch schedules, their
\(\mathsf{Good}_{23}\) bits,
the least-good selector, and the all-bad bit have a witness-independent joint
distribution.  The same holds for the first good completed attempt and the
internal cap-17 \(\mathsf{Abort}\) event.  This event does not include
truncation caused by a wall-clock deadline.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The three typed label-44 inputs extend one common state by distinct bytes.
At fresh random-oracle inputs their outputs are independent and uniform,
conditioned on the common prefix.  \(\mathsf{Good}_{23}\) is a deterministic function of the
resulting public schedule, so the Good bits and least-good selector inherit
this witness-independent law.  Fresh attempt entropy and a fresh burned nonce
make attempts independent.  Iterating the same coupling through the first
good attempt, or through all 17 failures, proves the claim.  Since Abort is
retained in the output space, no conditioning or division by a success
probability occurs.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Private-Merkle and EPRO hybrids}

For each opened leaf, the simulator samples the public 32-byte salt and
computes the typed leaf hash.  It assigns independent uniform labels to the
maximal unopened frontier subtrees and hashes upward to the public root.  A
bounded distinguisher detects this replacement only by querying a hidden
preimage or a programmed state, or through a collision.  The same lazy
programming method handles mask and salt expansion, transcript challenges,
and adaptive queries after output.

For a work predicate with \(g\) leading work bits, the honest miner publishes
the least successful 64-bit nonce.  The simulator samples the exact
truncated-geometric minimum, answers earlier nonces as failures, the minimum
as a success, and later queries lazily.  Across the six work states, the
hardest predicate has \(g=37\); failure of the 64-bit search space is at most
\(6\exp(-2^{27})\) per attempt.

The adjacent hybrids are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \(H_0\): the honest prover with real mask, salt, transcript, branch,
        retry, and work randomness;
  \item \(H_1\): replace the non-hash view by the exact affine-image sample;
  \item \(H_2\): replace hidden expander inputs and salted-leaf preimages,
        preserving all opened value--salt records;
  \item \(H_3\): sample unopened Merkle frontiers and program roots and
        transcript challenges at fresh typed inputs;
  \item \(H_4\): replace remaining hidden oracle states lazily, charge
        programmed-state collisions, and retain consistent post-output
        answers;
  \item \(H_5\): generate three schedules, Good bits, least-good selection,
        retries, and internal cap-exhaustion Abort using
        Lemma~\ref{lem:selection-hiding-abort};
  \item \(H_6\): replace the six work searches by their exact
        canonical-minimum laws;
  \item \(H_7\): serialize the schedule-dependent proof and append proof-
        account finalization, logs, framing, and deterministic state change;
  \item \(H_8\): the output of \(\mathsf{Sim}_{23}(x)\).
\end{enumerate}
The first step is perfect under
Assumption~\ref{lem:complete-affine-image}.  The
distribution changes in \(H_2\)--\(H_4\) are covered by one distinct-input
inventory rather than by counting repeated SHA-256 invocations.

\begin{lemma}[EPRO complete-view bound]
\label{lem:epro-complete-view}
Under Assumption~\ref{lem:complete-affine-image}, let \(A\le17\) be the
number of completed attempts.  Assume each attempt begins with
fresh independent 256-bit field entropy and leaf-salt seed and a fresh,
durably burned public nonce, and that only the fixed-boundary output in
Definition~\ref{def:profile23-complete-view} is public.  Assume also the
ideal-completion or witness-independent external-truncation boundary model
stated above.  Then
\begin{equation}
 \epsilon_{\rm RS}
 \le
 \frac{ACQ_H}{2^{256}}
 +\frac{\binom{AC}{2}}{2^{256}}
 +6A\exp(-2^{27}),
 \qquad C=969993.
 \label{eq:epro-bound}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Before a program point is exposed, the probability that at most \(Q_H\)
adaptive oracle queries contain any of the \(AC\) hidden inputs is at most
\(ACQ_H/2^{256}\).  This argument includes queries made after earlier public
messages because the still-hidden program points retain uniform 256-bit
states.  A union bound over pairs of programmed inputs gives
\(\binom{AC}{2}/2^{256}\) for internal or cross-family state collisions.
Outside those events, lazy programming makes every opened Merkle record,
frontier, root, transcript answer, and later oracle query identically
distributed in adjacent hybrids.  Lemma~\ref{lem:selection-hiding-abort}
handles the branch and retry law.  The canonical-work coupling is exact
unless one of the six 64-bit nonce spaces contains no solution; the hardest
37-bit predicate contributes at most \(\exp(-2^{64-37})\), yielding the last
term.  Proof-account framing and account mutation are deterministic functions
of the simulated proof, statement, and public account branch and therefore
add no error.
\end{proof}

At \(A=17\) and \(Q_H=2^{128}\), the three terms in
Equation~\eqref{eq:epro-bound} have respectively
\[
 104.02492234825198,\qquad
 209.04984478399368,\qquad
 193635243.91255844
\]
bits.  The first dominates; the sum has more than
104.0249223482519 bits at the displayed precision.

\begin{theorem}[Real view versus simulation]
\label{thm:real-vs-sim23}
Assume the complete affine-image and rank-coverage premise in
Assumption~\ref{lem:complete-affine-image}.  In the declared programmable
SHA-256 random-oracle model with ideal completion before the fixed boundary,
or with the witness-independent external truncation defined above, for
\(Q_H\le2^{128}\), every valid \((x,w)\), and arbitrary auxiliary input,
\[
 \mathsf{Real}_{23}(x,w)\approx_{\epsilon_{\rm RS}}
 \mathsf{Sim}_{23}(x),
 \qquad
 -\log_2\epsilon_{\rm RS}>104.0249223482519.
\]
Equivalently, the reported real-versus-simulator floor is 104.025 bits.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Compose Assumption~\ref{lem:complete-affine-image}, the hybrid sequence, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:epro-complete-view}.  The simulator is witness-free by its
construction, and both experiments include the same Abort outcome rather
than conditioning on success.  An independent external cutoff is coupled
identically and therefore adds no distinguishing advantage.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Pairwise-witness hiding]
\label{cor:pairwise-hiding}
Under the same affine-image premise and boundary model, for any fixed \(x\)
and any \(w_0,w_1\) satisfying \(R_{23}(x,w_i)=1\),
\[
 \epsilon_{\rm pair}\le2\epsilon_{\rm RS},
 \qquad
 -\log_2\epsilon_{\rm pair}>103.0249223482519.
\]
The reported pairwise floor is 103.025 bits.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply the triangle inequality along
\(
 \mathsf{Real}_{23}(x,w_0)\rightarrow
 \mathsf{Sim}_{23}(x)\rightarrow
 \mathsf{Real}_{23}(x,w_1)
\).
\end{proof}
