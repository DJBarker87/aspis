## Result

K1.6 is **blocked**, not closed.

The probability bookkeeping and an operational observed-proof experiment are
now kernel checked. The strongest theorem genuinely usable as the plain
classical-ROM foundation is BCS 2016, Theorem 7.1, or Block et al. 2023/2024,
Theorem 3.15 after an exact round-by-round-knowledge proof. Neither paper
supplies the protocol-specific reduction from the deployed Tag-73 duplex,
samplers, three positioned grinding loops, two truncated Merkle trees, and
first-cap-203 branch scan to its restricted state-restoration experiment.

The core missing statement for the plain compiler is

```text
FreshAcceptance(Tag73) \ LegalInteractiveTag73
  ⊆ ⋃ k, CompilerBadEvent(k).
```

Here `LegalInteractiveTag73` must be the exact interactive IOP execution
induced using one fixed adversary tape, one shared programmable SHA table, and
the exact byte schedule below. This is
`Tag73FreshAcceptanceTraceCover` in
[`V7FsAokCompiler.lean`](../../AspisFormal/AspisFormal/K1/V7FsAokCompiler.lean).
The legal set is not a freely supplied set: membership requires an
`InteractiveTag73Run` whose context matches the forgery, whose Q1 and tape
identity match the first run, whose replay checkpoints are nonempty and
same-tape, whose expected transcript/root-salt/candidate queries occur in one
shared oracle table, and whose resources meet the experiment's own budget.
Its adversary-query count equals the frozen Q1 length and its simulated-proof
count equals the actual observed history length, so the `Q_RO` and `q_sim`
factors cannot be supplied independently of the experiment.
The batch, fold, and final grinding counts are separately fixed to their
recorded probe lists, and the q16 branch count is the selected counter plus
one. Actor-specific query counts and programmed-point counts are tied to the
same shared oracle history/table, and legal membership excludes the recorded
programming, WUR, and transcript-coupling abort flags.
The cover is a named proposition and theorem hypothesis, not a field that
assumes the Fiat–Shamir conclusion. A formal countermodel proves that the
interactive K1.2–K1.5 theorem alone cannot imply the noninteractive bound.
For an observed/simulated-proof history, canonical simulation, programming
freshness, and WUR bounds are additional unresolved protocol lemmas; they are
enumerated in the raw expression rather than accepted as new external
cryptographic assumptions.

Subject to that trace-cover theorem, the following are proved:

```text
Pr[fresh Tag73 acceptance]
  ≤ L · Pr[valid extracted witness] + εTag73,raw

(Pr[fresh Tag73 acceptance] - εTag73,raw) / L
  ≤ Pr[valid extracted witness]                 (0 < L < ∞).
```

These are probabilistic statements. Nothing claims that every individual
accepting proof deterministically yields a witness.

## Exact SHA duplex

Let `S` be the current 32-byte transcript state, initially zero. The deployed
primitive is:

```text
absorb(label, data): S := SHA256(S || 0x00 || label || data)
squeeze block:       out := SHA256(S || 0x01)
                     S   := SHA256(S || 0x02)
grind(nonceLE):      SHA256(S || 0x03 || nonceLE)  -- S is unchanged
```

Every challenge sampler consumes a sequential stream of squeeze blocks; a
rejected field word advances that same stream. Thus a challenge is bound to
the complete preceding transcript state, but challenge calls are not assumed
independent.

The ordinary QM31 sampler uses at most four blocks: four limbs, eight candidate
31-bit words per limb, eight words per block. The nonzero-QM31 and secure-circle
samplers each make at most three outer attempts and therefore consume at most
twelve blocks. Sampler exhaustion rejects the proof and is an explicit event,
not an unbounded ideal sample.

## Binding and retry claims proved in Lean

[`V7Tag73TranscriptSchedule.lean`](../../AspisFormal/AspisFormal/K1/V7Tag73TranscriptSchedule.lean)
proves syntactically, before any cryptographic assumption:

- every absorb hashes the exact previous 32-byte state, domain byte, label, and
  payload;
- every squeeze hashes the exact previous state under `0x01` and advances it
  under `0x02`;
- grinding hashes `state || 0x03 || nonceLE` without advancing `state`;
- each challenge starts from the state obtained by running exactly its event
  prefix;
- C2 absorption occurs after `lambda` and `chi`;
- the three work stages are separate and none has a first-success policy;
- every earlier q16 counter is sampled and noncompact, while the chosen
  schedule is compact;
- all counter branches start from one post-final-nonce base, and only the
  selected branch continues;
- `rho` is sampled from the state after the selected branch; and
- terminal query acceptance exposes only the one batched residual equality.

Concluding that two different byte histories cannot share a state is exactly
the SHA-256/random-oracle boundary; it is not asserted by these syntactic
theorems.

## Observed-proof extraction experiment

[`V7FsAokExperiment.lean`](../../AspisFormal/AspisFormal/K1/V7FsAokExperiment.lean)
contains the operational game, not a one-proof decoder:

- one finite master PMF fixes all first-run, simulator, verifier, replay, and
  extractor coins;
- every oracle table entry and ordered query is recorded, with actor
  `adversary`, `simulator`, `verifier`, or `extractorReplay`;
- `Q1` is frozen from adversary calls when the first run halts; later verifier
  and extractor calls do not retroactively enter `Q1`;
- programming is a separate operation and aborts if the input was previously
  defined, even with the same output;
- fresh queries are controlled by a function of the complete ordered history
  and the current input;
- a restart capability closes over one fixed random tape and checkpoint. The
  extractor can request adaptive replay answers but cannot replace the tape;
- oracle/program/query, fuel, runtime, restart, three grinding-query, q16
  branch, verifier, and simulated-proof budgets are explicit;
- a checked Markov inequality converts an expected natural-number cost into
  the explicit probability of exceeding a nonzero hard cutoff;
- acceptance, resource abort, programming abort, WUR failure, transcript
  coupling failure, and valid witness extraction are separate events; and
- the extractor structure contains only an algorithm. It has no
  `compiledBound`, `extract_sound`, or conclusion-shaped field.

Freshness has two levels. Ganesh et al.'s observed target requires pair
freshness `(instance, proof)`. A globally fresh attempt/proof-account ID is
strictly stronger, and Lean proves `AttemptFresh -> PairFresh`. Production
binds the proof account into the precommit and both root salts, but a finalized
account can be submitted again. Therefore deployed Tag 73 does **not** by
itself establish global attempt freshness relative to an arbitrary simulated
history. The observed-proof route needs FS-WUR unless the surrounding game
adds and proves that stronger freshness condition.

The older `ClassicalFSCompiler` is not an FS proof: its `compiledBound` member
is already the desired inequality. Likewise, the Pool
`KnowledgeExtractorInterface` only types an extractor boundary; a public proof
and one observed SHA query log do not construct the same-randomness black-box
replays required by BCS or Ganesh. Neither interface is used to discharge the
new trace cover.

### BCS 2016

[Ben-Sasson, Chiesa and Spooner, *Interactive Oracle Proofs*](https://eprint.iacr.org/2016/116.pdf):

- Section 2.3 defines the noninteractive proof-of-knowledge extractor as
  strict PPT, black-box, and same-randomness restarting.
- Definition 5.1 distinguishes state-restoration soundness from
  state-restoration **proof of knowledge**.
- Section 5.4 permits restricted restoration except returning to the empty
  verifier state after the first iteration.
- Lemma 7.3 supplies the ROM-to-state-restoration coupling loss.
- Theorem 7.1 gives, for a `Q`-query adversary,

  ```text
  e_FS-k(x,Q,lambda)
    = e_sr-k(x,Q) + 3(Q^2 + 1) / 2^lambda.
  ```

- Lemma 3.2 is only the ordinary full-output binary-Merkle extraction lemma;
  it does not cover two shared, typed, 208-bit trees.

This is the strongest defensible foundation for plain classical-ROM Tag-73
AoK. Ordinary soundness cannot replace `e_sr-k`, and no soundness probability
is divided by adversary work.

### Block et al. 2023/2024

[Block et al., *Fiat–Shamir Security of FRI and Related SNARKs*](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/1071.pdf):

- Definition 3.13 is algorithmic round-by-round knowledge: the extractor gets
  the index, statement, partial transcript, and next-message black box.
- Definition 3.14 is the BCS-style NIROP knowledge experiment.
- Theorem 3.15 gives

  ```text
  epsilon_FS-k(x,Q,kappa)
    = Q * epsilon_RBR-k(x) + 3(Q^2 + 1) / 2^kappa.
  ```

- Theorem 5.11 proves RBR knowledge only for the paper's exact FRI and
  Batched-FRI relations. It extracts the first FRI oracle as a close word.

A later oracle commitment after earlier public coins is structurally allowed,
so C2-after-`lambda,chi` is not itself disqualifying. The paper does not prove
RBR knowledge for Tag-73's semantic relation, samplers, grinding, Merkle
grammar, or witness relation.

[Block and Tiwari, *On the Concrete Security of Non-interactive FRI*](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1161.pdf)
is soundness-only. Its work-normalized `Q/epsilon` metric is not extraction
failure and its single FRI grinding analysis cannot merge Tag-73's three
positioned nonce stages.

### CMS 2019

[Chiesa, Manohar and Spooner, *Succinct Arguments in the Quantum Random Oracle
Model*](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/834.pdf):

- Definitions 8.3–8.5 define state, round-by-round soundness, and algorithmic
  round-by-round knowledge.
- Theorem 8.6 gives QROM soundness
  `O(t^2 epsilon + t^3/2^lambda)` and extraction probability
  `Omega(mu - t^2 kappa - t^3/2^lambda)`.
- Proposition 8.14 contains the more explicit instability term
  `kappa + (2t+1)/2^lambda`.
- Theorem B.3 treats the stronger knowledge condition needed by the original
  BCS chain.

CMS is quantum, asymptotic with hidden constants, and not the requested exact
classical-ROM theorem.

### Ganesh et al. 2024

[Ganesh et al., *Fiat–Shamir Bulletproofs are Non-Malleable*](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/147.pdf):

- Definition 2.8 is the relevant adaptive FS-EXT experiment. The first run
  outputs `(x,T)` under tape `rho`; extraction receives `(pp,x,T,rho,Q1)` and
  black-box next-message access.
- Its guarantee is probabilistic:
  `ext >= (acc - kappa(lambda,q))/poly(lambda)`.
- Theorem 2.1 is a multi-round special-soundness route, but as stated uses a
  common challenge set.
- Definition 3.1 defines strong FS-SIM-EXT with pair freshness
  `(x,T) notin Q2`.
- Definitions 3.2 and 3.3 define SR-WUR and FS-WUR.
- Equation (3) isolates the shared-prefix programming bad event used in the
  WUR reduction.
- Lemma 3.1 gives
  `Adv_FS-WUR <= Adv_SR-WUR + (q_RO+1)/|Ch_min|`.
- Lemma 3.2 gives the observed-proof raw error
  `q_sim * Adv_FS-WUR + kappa`.

The paper assumes a canonical programmable-ROM simulator obtained from
perfect HVZK. Computational hiding alone is not this premise. No existing V7
result constructs such a simulator, proves its programming-abort bound, or
establishes Tag-73 WUR. Consequently the observed-proof extension remains
uninstantiated even after the plain adapter is proved.

## Exact symbolic raw error

Let `Q_RO` be the first-run adversary random-oracle query bound, `q_sim` the
number of prior simulated proofs, and `|Ch_min|` the smallest challenge domain
only if the Ganesh common-domain/canonical-simulation hypotheses are proved.
The Lean expression is exactly:

```text
εTag73,raw =
    εK1.2,two-tree-208
  + εK1.3,circle-list
  + εK1.4,coherent-chain
  + εK1.5,witness
  + 3(Q_RO^2 + 1) / 2^256
  + εC2-after-lambda-chi
  + εgrind-35
  + εgrind-31
  + εgrind-34
  + εsampler-QM31
  + εsampler-nonzero-QM31
  + εsampler-secure-circle
  + εfirst-cap203-q16
  + εduplex-state
  + εQ1-separation
  + εcanonical-simulator
  + εprogramming-collision
  + q_sim * (AdvSR-WUR + (Q_RO+1)/|Ch_min|)
  + εprogram/release/statement/attempt-binding
  + εresource-budget.
```

This is a raw union-bound sum and may exceed one. It is not divided by work.
The 35-, 31-, and 34-bit terms are separate stage-coupling events; they are
not three independent Bernoulli assumptions. The 256-bit BCS term is the exact
published full-hash compiler term. It becomes applicable to the custom duplex
only after the trace adapter is proved. The two-tree 208-bit term remains the
exact K1.2 input and is intentionally **not** replaced by a generic 104-bit
birthday slogan.

If Block Theorem 3.15 is used instead of a monolithic BCS state-restoration
knowledge error, the upstream interactive error must include its exact
`Q_RO * epsilon_RBR-k`; it must not be silently added twice.

## Explicit K1.2–K1.5 premises

`UpstreamK12ToK15KnowledgeBound` may be instantiated only after supplying all
of the following for the legal interactive experiment:

1. K1.2: the two-tree, shared-topology, typed-leaf, 208-bit query-graph
   extractor and its exact failure event;
2. K1.3: the published circle-code/list-decoding theorem with every deployed
   rate, distance, multiplicity, and proximity side condition;
3. K1.4: selection of one coherent candidate across semantic, OOD, query,
   and four relation boundaries;
4. K1.5: deterministic recovery of a witness satisfying the deployed spend
   relation from that coherent chain; and
5. a bounded/expected-polynomial interactive state-restoration extractor with
   explicit oracle-query, restart, and runtime loss `L`.

None of these premises may be ordinary soundness. The K1.2–K1.5 theorem's
left side is `Pr[legal interactive execution]`, not `Pr[Fiat–Shamir accepts]`.

## Smallest remaining proof

For every bounded Tag-73 adversary, fix its auxiliary input and random tape.
In the joint first-run/replay world, and outside the explicitly named bad
events, prove all of the following in one trace reduction:

1. every deployed SHA call uses the same lazy programmable table;
2. `Q1` is exactly the first-run adversary history at halt;
3. replay starts from the same hidden tape and the requested nonempty
   checkpoint, while answers may depend on the ordered history/current query;
4. the program ID, release, statement digest, proof-account attempt, C1, then
   `lambda,chi`, then adaptive C2 are preserved byte for byte;
5. bounded sequential field, nonzero-field, circle, and q16 samplers are
   coupled to raw duplex blocks, including exhaustion aborts;
6. each 35/31/34-bit nonce retry is charged only at its actual saved state;
7. all 64 q16 candidates clone the post-final-nonce state and the accepted
   branch is exactly the first cap-203 branch;
8. the two 208-bit typed authentication graphs and degree-15 `rho`-batched
   residual are the interactive verifier messages/checks expected by K1.2;
9. simulator programming collisions, WUR, and resource exhaustion map to the
   corresponding raw events; and
10. outside those events, the resulting object is a legal restricted
    state-restoration IOP execution.

No audited paper proves this statement for Tag 73. It is narrower than the
desired AoK inequality and mentions neither a witness nor extraction success,
so proving it does not assume the conclusion.

## Minimal ways forward

The smallest proof-only change is to implement the trace adapter above over
raw 256-bit random-oracle blocks and prove its coupling event by event. No
deployed protocol change is required for plain AoK.

For observed proofs, one additional route is necessary:

- prove perfect-HVZK canonical simulation plus Tag-73 FS-WUR and programming
  freshness, then instantiate Ganesh Lemmas 3.1–3.2; or
- strengthen the application experiment/protocol so the target proof account
  is globally fresh relative to every simulated proof, and prove a new-domain
  separation lemma from the attempt absorption before C1. The deployed
  verifier alone does not enforce this history-relative property.

To invoke BCS literally without a custom adapter would require a material
protocol revision to the standard BCS full-output hash/Merkle chain and a
fixed unconditioned raw public-coin schedule. That is not claimed as necessary
for security; it is only the minimal route to verbatim theorem syntax.

## Trusted boundaries after this work

The intended final chain may keep only these cryptographic/system assumptions
external:

- SHA-256 as the required random oracle and collision/preimage primitive;
- Poseidon security in the upstream spend relation;
- the published circle-code/list-decoding theorem;
- Solana runtime/SBF correspondence; and
- pinned Charon/Aeneas translation correctness as a named trusted-tool
  boundary.

K1.2–K1.5 remain explicit modular premises while their dedicated work is in
progress. No `ClassicalFSCompiler.compiledBound`, public-proof-plus-one-query
decoder, conclusion-shaped axiom, `sorry`, or `admit` is introduced.
