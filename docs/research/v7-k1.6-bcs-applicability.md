# V7 K1.6: BCS 2016 applicability gate for deployed Tag-73

Status: **go/no-go audit only; this document does not close K1.6**.

## Result

The deployed Tag-73 verifier is **not a literal instance** of the compiler in
Ben-Sasson--Chiesa--Spooner (BCS), Section 6. Consequently BCS Theorem 7.1
cannot be cited directly for Tag-73 knowledge soundness, and its error term
`3 (m^2 + 1) 2^-lambda` cannot be copied into the V7 ledger.

There are two distinct first mismatches:

1. At the byte/state-machine level, the mismatch is already present at the
   first challenge after `C1`. BCS derives a verifier message from
   `rho1(x || sigma_i)` and advances its chain only by
   `sigma_i = rho2(root_i || sigma_{i-1})`. Tag-73 instead derives each raw
   block as `H(S || 0x01)` and, independently, advances to
   `H(S || 0x02)`, after a sequence of framed absorbs. Thus the `alpha(sigma)`
   invariant used in Lemma 7.3 does not apply to the first `lambda` squeeze.
2. At the underlying-protocol type level, the first grinding check exposes a
   second mismatch. The public-coin IOP accepted by BCS has no access to the
   compiler random oracle. If Tag's verifier-checked predicate
   `lz(H(S || 0x03 || nonce)) >= z` is put inside the underlying IOP, that
   object is not the IOP of Sections 4--5. If it is kept in the compiler, the
   deployed compiler is an extension of Section 6 and needs a new reduction.

For an ordinary, unconditional PoK inequality there is a sound simplification:
drop each leading-zero *check*, but retain the adversarial nonce, its exact
absorb, and every later transcript operation. Acceptance by the real verifier
is then a pointwise subset of acceptance by the check-erased verifier. This
means the predicates themselves need not force an auxiliary-RO IOP extension.
It does **not** erase nonce grinding freedom, reduce the adversary query budget,
or repair the non-BCS transcript compiler.

Challenge-dependent `C2` is not itself an obstruction: an IOP prover message
may depend on every preceding public verifier message. The obstruction is the
missing reduction from Tag-73's exact random-oracle query graph to the
restricted state-restoration experiment.

## What BCS actually proves

Section 2.3 fixes an instance `x` and an `m`-query oracle prover. Its
extractor receives `(x, 1^m, 1^lambda)` and controls answers to the prover's
last random-oracle query. On output, the prover restarts “with the same
randomness and any auxiliary inputs” (p. 13). Its code, tape, and auxiliary
input remain hidden. This is a classical, black-box, same-tape restart
interface; it is not extraction from one proof and one recorded query log.

Definition 5.1 separately requires a restricted-state-restoration knowledge
extractor for the underlying public-coin IOP. Proof of ordinary soundness does
not supply that extractor. The paper also chooses strict, rather than expected,
probabilistic polynomial time (footnote 4, p. 19).

Section 5.4 says a restricted prover “cannot restore the verifier to the empty
state” after the first iteration (p. 23). It may restore any other complete
previously seen state. Every restoration asks the verifier for a fresh next
uniform message. The budget counts all such prover rounds over the entire
branching interaction, not the depth of one transcript.

Theorem 7.1 applies to the exact Section 6 transformation `T(P,V)`. Its PoK
conclusion is:

```
e_FS(x,m,lambda) = ebar_sr(x,m) + 3 (m^2 + 1) 2^-lambda.
```

Here `ebar_sr` is already the **restricted state-restoration proof-of-knowledge
error**, not ordinary IOP soundness. Lemma 7.3 is the compiler simulation
lemma: an accepting `m`-query NIROP prover yields a restricted
state-restoration prover using `b <= m` rounds and loses at most
`3 (m^2 + 1) 2^-lambda`. Section 7.3 then runs the assumed underlying
state-restoration extractor against that constructed prover. Theorem 7.1 does
not extract a witness merely from acceptance.

The proof of Lemma 7.3 is specialized to the Section 6 grammar:

- `rho` is split by prefixes into `rho1` (verifier coins) and `rho2`
  (Merkle trees and root chaining), and queries to both count toward `m`;
- `sigma_0 = rho2(x)`;
- every IOP verifier message is `rho1(x || sigma_{i-1})`;
- every prover oracle is committed by the paper's full-output Merkle scheme;
- every chain transition is `sigma_i = rho2(root_i || sigma_{i-1})`;
- the reduction maps `sigma_i` to one complete verifier state and invokes
  Valiant extraction at the corresponding root query.

The three error contributions in Lemma 7.3 cover this particular linear chain,
cross-suboracle ordering/collisions, and this particular Merkle extractor.
They are not a theorem about arbitrary domain-separated SHA transcript DAGs.

## Interpretation labels

The matrix uses three deliberately different judgments:

- **Literal**: Tag-73, unchanged, is the Section 6 output `T(P,V)`, so the
  published theorem can be applied without changing its proof.
- **Conservative extension**: the deployed protocol need not change, but a new
  generalization of Lemma 7.3 must be proved. “Plausible” is not a theorem.
- **Deterministic refinement**: source-level proofs can reconstruct a challenge
  or acceptance result from a complete oracle table/log. This is useful, but
  it neither couples a random execution to state restoration nor extracts a
  witness.

## Can the three work predicates be erased?

Yes, for the standard BCS-style PoK inequality, but only in a precise sense.
Let `V_pow` be the real verifier and let `V_free` be byte-for-byte identical
except that its three leading-zero tests always pass. In particular,
`V_free` still parses the same three nonces, absorbs them at the same three
states, derives all later challenges from those absorbs, and performs every
other rejection check. For every fixed oracle, instance, and proof,

```
1[V_pow^H(x,pi) accepts] <= 1[V_free^H(x,pi) accepts].
```

Therefore, if an extractor `E` has the unconditional PoK guarantee

```
Pr[R(x,E^A)] >= Pr[V_free^H(x,A^H) accepts] - e,
```

then the same extractor and error also satisfy the inequality with `V_pow` on
the right. This is pure monotonicity; it needs no independence claim. It also
works for all three predicates simultaneously.

What cannot be erased is the nonce-selection power. A malicious prover may
query grind, absorb, squeeze, or still later inputs for many candidate nonces
and select using everything it has learned. Domain bytes `0x03` (grind),
`0x00` (absorb), `0x01` (squeeze), and `0x02` (advance) make the corresponding
well-formed inputs distinct, but domain separation alone does not make an
*adaptively selected* downstream challenge independent of the adversary's
view. The generalized state-restoration/query-DAG proof must account for that
selection and charge all queries to `Q`.

Thus the recommended conservative route does **not** put PoW inside the
underlying IOP and does not need an auxiliary random oracle merely to check
leading zeros. An auxiliary/same-RO extension is needed only if the theorem is
intended to preserve exact acceptance equivalence or claim a tighter security
benefit from the work factors. The monotonic argument also does not by itself
justify a theorem stated conditionally on the distribution of one already
observed accepting proof; that observed-proof experiment needs its own
unconditional joint-event formulation or a separate conditioning proof.

## Exact meaning of `m`, local `Q`, and `q(x)`

These quantities must not be conflated.

- In BCS Section 2.1, `NumQueries(A,rho)` is the number of random-oracle calls
  made by `A^rho`. An algorithm is `m`-query only if that number is at most
  `m` for **every** oracle. Thus `m` is a strict per-run cap, not an expected
  work value. Duplicate calls still are calls, even though the proof's tables
  answer them consistently.
- For Theorem 7.1, `m` counts calls made by the malicious compiled prover to
  both prefix suboracles. It is not the proof length, not the number of
  successful nonce trials, not `2^35+2^31+2^34`, and not the verifier's
  verification cost. The verifier may itself query the shared oracle while
  checking the proof; those calls are not part of the definition of an
  `m`-query prover.
- `q(x)` in Sections 4 and 6 is the underlying IOP verifier's number of
  random-access queries to prover oracles. It appears in proof length and
  running time, not as the adversary budget in the compiler error.
- If V7 uses `Q` as the analogue of `m`, it should mean an explicit strict
  bound on **every adversarial SHA-256/ROM call in one restarted run**. This
  includes every absorb, squeeze/advance pair, rejected sampler block, nonce
  trial, Merkle computation, or rejected q16 clone that the adversary itself
  evaluates, plus unrelated prequeries permitted by the experiment. Calls
  made only by the verifier are a separate verifier budget; including them in
  a joint `Q_total` is possible, but then `Q_total` is not BCS's `m` and the
  conversion must be stated. One cannot count only adversarial calls that
  later appear in an accepting transcript.
- Lemma 7.3 obtains `b <= m` because its constructed prover sends at most one
  IOP message in response to each adversary query. This is a fact about the
  Section 6 chain. For Tag-73, `b <= Q` (or any alternative constant-factor
  relation) is an obligation of the missing query-DAG simulation lemma, not a
  definition.
- Section 2.3's restart gives each re-execution the same adversary random tape
  and auxiliary input. The extractor selects oracle replies as execution
  proceeds. A public proof plus a single passive query history is strictly
  weaker than this interface.

## Restoration restrictions for an exact Tag model

The state-restoration game stores complete public verifier transcripts. It
does not give the prover arbitrary snapshots of CPU memory or arbitrary SHA
states. For a Tag-specific reduction, a map from every live transcript state
`S` to a previously seen complete verifier state must be proved. In
particular:

1. the initial modeled IOP round must produce a nonempty complete verifier
   state before `C1` can be retried;
2. both outputs associated with a squeeze must be consistently linked to that
   state without revealing future coins before the corresponding prover move;
3. a restore may target only a state already in `SeenStates`;
4. restoring a state causes the real IOP verifier to sample a fresh next
   message; it does not let the reduction choose an arbitrary conditioned
   sampler result;
5. q16 clones are branches from one prior complete state, not 64 sequential
   advances of that state; and
6. the three nonce-search families branch at three different complete states.

Any interface that simply postulates a `compiledBound`, an accepting rewind,
or the desired witness probability would be circular.

## Smallest missing mathematical statement

The smallest honest bridge is a protocol-specific replacement for BCS Lemma
7.3, not a new conclusion-shaped compiler assumption. One suitable statement
shape is:

> For every fixed, fully bound Tag-73 instance `x` and strict `Q`-query
> same-tape black-box ROM prover `A`, construct a restricted-state-restoring
> prover `A_sr` for an explicitly defined ROM-free expanded V7 IOP, with an
> explicit round bound `b(Q)`, such that its acceptance probability is at
> least `Pr_H[V_Tag^H(x,A^H)=1]` minus a sum of named raw query-graph failure
> events.

The proof must establish, rather than assume:

- exact distributional coupling for absorb, two-call squeeze, and each
  bounded rejection sampler;
- a legal complete-verifier-state map and restoration schedule;
- separate branching/accounting for the 35-, 31-, and 34-bit work stages;
- freshness/order for every accepted and rejected q16 branch and exact
  first-success selection;
- fixed-statement/attempt freshness, or an explicit adaptive loss;
- integration with the exact two-tree 208-bit K1.2 extractor; and
- a raw failure expression separating 256-bit transcript events, 208-bit
  typed Merkle events, unqueried predictions, and query-DAG ordering/collision
  events. Bounded sampler exhaustion and all-q16-fail are verifier rejection
  (and honest-completeness) events; they become extraction-loss terms only if
  a proposed coupling conditions them away.

Only after this lemma is proved may it be composed with a genuine restricted
state-restoration PoK premise for K1.2--K1.5. The conclusion then follows in
the same *pattern* as BCS Section 7.3, but not by literal application of
Theorem 7.1.

## Go/no-go and minimal changes

**Literal BCS Theorem 7.1 instantiation: NO-GO.** The first trace-level
incompatibility is the initial absorb/two-call-squeeze compiler grammar; the
first model-level incompatibility encountered when preserving grinding is
compiler-RO access outside the Section 6 transform.

**Protocol-preserving proof route: possible but unproved.** Prove the
Tag-73 query-DAG-to-restricted-state-restoration lemma above. This is a
conservative extension of the technique of Lemma 7.3, not a citation to its
published statement. Exact Rust/Lean transcript refinements can discharge the
deterministic half but cannot substitute for the coupling lemma.

**Minimal protocol change for literal BCS use:** replace the deployed
transcript by the exact Section 6 construction: a fixed pre-oracle statement,
separate prefixed challenge/hash roles, fixed-length uniform verifier
messages, one full-output linear root chain, and the BCS Merkle grammar;
remove the three same-RO work predicates and cloned first-success sampler, or
place them behind separately proved transformations. That is a material wire
change and therefore is not a repair for already deployed Tag-73.

No impossibility of a classical-ROM Tag-73 AoK theorem is claimed. What is
ruled out is the unsupported shortcut from the published BCS statement to the
deployed transcript.
